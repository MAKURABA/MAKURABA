PROTO-ATOMOS

PROTO-ATOMOS —

The Magnitude-Scalar, Polarity, and Re cursive Triangulated Manifold Conjecture

Abstract

Proto-Atomos is proposed as a unified mathematical and conceptual instrument for representing, investigating, and reconstructing the structure of knowable things.

The central conjecture is that an object, substance, concept, process, or system can be represented as a bounded and recursively extensible configuration of distinguishable qualities. Each quality may possess a magnitude, a conceptual polarity, and relationships with other qualities. These qualities can be arranged into triangular relational structures, and those structures can be connected into a larger manifold-like representation.

The resulting configuration is not necessarily a depiction of the physical shape of the subject. It is a geometric representation of its distinguishable properties, relationships, states, and observable transformations.

The primitive identity is represented by Δ = Δ. The Δ-position establishes the reference identity and boundary of the configuration. An observing agent is represented within that boundary rather than treated as an external, universally neutral source of information.

Magnitude and polarity are separate quantities. Magnitude expresses the degree to which a quality is present, expressed, or realized within a defined model. Polarity expresses the position of that quality between two defined conceptual reference conditions.

The qualities can be represented as scalar-coordinate pairs, organized into vectors, triangulated through relationships, and recursively connected into a larger geometric construction.

The conjecture proposes that this construction can serve as a common representational foundation for information encoding, inference, comparison, object description, state reconstruction, and conjecture generation.

PART I — THE FOUNDATIONAL IDENTITY

  1. The Proto-Atomos Principle

Proto-Atomos is proposed as a primitive representational instrument capable of constructing a structured description of something from its distinguishable qualities and relationships.

The term does not designate an ordinary physical atom or a smallest material particle.

It designates a proposed primitive unit of conceptual and mathematical construction: a system capable of taking distinguishable information and organizing it into a coherent, relationally defined configuration.

Its purpose is to establish a common structure through which otherwise different subjects can be represented.

A physical substance, an organism, a mathematical proposition, a psychological state, a machine, and an abstract concept may differ fundamentally in their material constitution. Nevertheless, each can be examined through distinguishable qualities, relationships, magnitudes, states, and transformations.

Proto-Atomos proposes a common representational architecture for doing so.

The conjecture is not that every subject possesses identical qualities. It is that sufficiently defined subjects can be represented through a common geometric construction.

  1. The Primitive Identity: Δ = Δ

The primitive identity of Proto-Atomos is expressed as:

Δ = Δ

This expression establishes the identity-reference principle.

Δ is not automatically equivalent to an ordinary numerical zero, a physical point, a particle, an empty set, or a material object.

It represents the primitive identity condition from which a configuration can be distinguished, referenced, and reconstructed.

Three concepts must remain distinct:

  • Δ: the primitive identity-reference.
  • Δ-position: the position or reference condition through which a configuration is established.
  • Δ-boundary: the bounded geometric domain within which the configuration and its observation are represented.

The Δ-boundary is depicted as a triangular enclosure in the elementary geometric model.

The triangle is not necessarily the physical shape of the subject. It is the geometric boundary of its representational configuration.

The identity-reference remains Δ; the triangular boundary is the geometric expression of that reference within this model.

  1. The Observer Within the Boundary

The observing agent is included within the Δ-bounded configuration.

This is a foundational feature rather than a decorative element.

The observer is not assumed to possess unrestricted access to the complete subject. The observer occupies a position within the representational system and encounters the subject through available information, measurement, interpretation, and interaction.

The observer therefore has a describable state of their own.

The configuration must distinguish:

  1. The subject being represented.
  2. The qualities attributed to that subject.
  3. The observer examining those qualities.
  4. The information available to the observer.
  5. The relationships inferred from that information.

The observer's position may affect the resulting description without necessarily changing the subject itself.

This distinction is essential.

A change in the observer's interpretation is not automatically a change in the object. Equally, a change in the object may occur without being detected by the observer.

Proto-Atomos therefore treats observation as an internal relational component of the representation.

PART II — THE MAGNITUDE-SCALAR

  1. The Magnitude Principle

The magnitude-scalar is the first quantitative component of the quality representation.

Let Mᵢ represent the magnitude assigned to a particular quality qᵢ.

For a normalized model:

0 ≤ Mᵢ ≤ 1

Where:

  • Mᵢ = 0 represents the defined lower or absent condition within the model.
  • Mᵢ = 1 represents the defined maximum or reference condition.
  • 0 < Mᵢ < 1 represents an intermediate degree.

Magnitude expresses degree, intensity, quantity, extent, or another explicitly defined measure of a quality.

The meaning of magnitude depends upon the quality being examined.

For example:

A temperature-related quality may use a normalized magnitude to represent the degree of thermal intensity relative to a specified reference interval.

A structural quality may use magnitude to represent the degree of rigidity.

A conceptual quality may use magnitude to represent the degree to which a particular attribute is expressed within the model.

A psychological quality may use magnitude to represent a measured or operationally defined degree of an observable state.

These examples do not imply that all such qualities can be measured using the same empirical instrument. They illustrate that the mathematical representation can be shared while the measurement procedure remains subject-specific.

4.1 Magnitude is not polarity

Magnitude and polarity must never be treated as interchangeable.

Magnitude answers:

How much of a defined quality is present or expressed?

Polarity answers:

Where does that quality stand between its defined conceptual reference conditions?

A quality may have high magnitude and a polarity near either endpoint.

A quality may have low magnitude and a polarity near either endpoint.

A quality may have an intermediate magnitude and a polarity near the midpoint.

Therefore:

Mᵢ ≠ Pᵢ

Magnitude is a scalar quantity. Polarity is a separate normalized coordinate.

Their combination produces a more informative state description.

PART III — THE POLARITY PRINCIPLE

  1. Conceptual Polarity

Proto-Atomos proposes that a quality may possess conceptual polarity.

A polarity is defined by two distinguishable reference conditions.

These conditions are designated as the Monomaxum points of that polarity.

Let Pᵢ represent the polarity coordinate of a quality.

For a normalized dual-polarity system:

0 ≤ Pᵢ ≤ 1

The endpoints are:

Pᵢ = 0 — first Monomaxum reference condition.

Pᵢ = 1 — second Monomaxum reference condition.

The intermediate interval represents positions between those conditions.

The endpoints do not inherently mean good and bad, positive and negative, desirable and undesirable, or true and false.

Their meanings depend upon the quality and the reference conditions established for that quality.

Examples of possible conceptual polarities include:

  • Expansion ↔ contraction.
  • Attraction ↔ repulsion.
  • Flexible ↔ rigid.
  • Open ↔ closed.
  • Inclusion ↔ exclusion.
  • Presence ↔ absence.
  • Stability ↔ instability.
  • Compression ↔ extension.
  • Activation ↔ inhibition.

These are examples of possible representational axes, not claims that every pair is a perfect physical or logical opposite.

The definition of each axis must specify what its endpoints mean.

  1. The Polarity Coordinate

A normalized polarity coordinate can be converted into a signed representation:

Sᵢ = 2Pᵢ − 1

Therefore:

Pᵢ = 0 → Sᵢ = −1

Pᵢ = 0.5 → Sᵢ = 0

Pᵢ = 1 → Sᵢ = +1

This conversion allows polarity to be represented using either a zero-to-one interval or a negative-to-positive coordinate.

The two representations are mathematically interchangeable when the endpoint definitions remain consistent.

The midpoint does not necessarily indicate neutrality in a philosophical or physical sense. It indicates the midpoint of the selected coordinate interval.

  1. Polarity Is Not Necessarily Binary in the Complete Object

A subject may contain multiple qualities, each with its own polarity.

An object is not required to possess only two qualities, nor must its complete identity be reducible to one pair of opposites.

A system may contain:

  • Multiple independent dual-polarity axes.
  • Qualities that are not meaningfully dual-polar.
  • Qualities with multiple distinguishable reference conditions.
  • Relationships between several polarities.
  • Qualities whose polarity changes depending on context.

The dual-polarity model is therefore an elementary construction, not a universal restriction on the number of properties a subject may possess.

Where a quality has more than two meaningful reference conditions, its representation must be extended to a multidimensional or categorical coordinate system rather than forcing it into an unsuitable binary axis.

The principle is that polarity is a relational coordinate defined by reference conditions, not a declaration that reality contains only two possible states.

PART IV — THE VECTOR STATE

  1. The Quality-State Pair

Each quality can be represented by a state pair:

Qᵢ = (Mᵢ, Pᵢ)

Where:

Mᵢ = magnitude of quality i.

Pᵢ = polarity coordinate of quality i.

This pair establishes a minimal quantitative description of a quality that possesses both a defined magnitude and a defined dual-polarity axis.

For example:

Q₁ = (0.80, 0.20)

This describes a quality with a normalized magnitude of 0.80 and a polarity position of 0.20.

It does not mean that the quality is 80% positive or 20% present. Those interpretations would require separate definitions.

The pair describes two independent dimensions.

  1. The Vector-State System

A subject containing multiple qualities can be represented as a collection of quality-state pairs.

V = [Q₁, Q₂, Q₃, ... Qₙ]

Or, expanded:

V = [(M₁,P₁), (M₂,P₂), (M₃,P₃), ... (Mₙ,Pₙ)]

This is the quality-state vector representation.

The vector is not a single magnitude or a single polarity. It is an organized collection of scalar-coordinate pairs.

Each pair describes one quality.

The resulting structure permits a subject to possess many distinguishable qualities without collapsing them into one number.

For example, a hypothetical object might be described through:

  • Rigidity.
  • Flexibility.
  • Thermal intensity.
  • Structural integrity.
  • Expansion.
  • Compression.
  • Reflectivity.
  • Permeability.

Each quality can have its own magnitude and, where appropriate, polarity.

The resulting vector-state system is a structured description of the subject.

  1. Independence and Dependence Between Qualities

Qualities must initially be treated as distinguishable variables.

However, distinguishability does not imply complete independence.

Some qualities may be correlated.

Some may be causally dependent.

Some may be mutually exclusive.

Some may reinforce one another.

Some may change together because they share an underlying mechanism.

Others may remain independent.

Proto-Atomos therefore distinguishes the existence of a quality from its relationships with other qualities.

A relationship between Qᵢ and Qⱼ can be represented separately:

Rᵢⱼ = relationship(Qᵢ,Qⱼ)

The relationship may describe association, dependency, opposition, influence, equivalence, constraint, or another defined connection.

The relationship must not be assumed to be causal merely because two qualities change together.

Causality requires additional evidence or a separately established causal model.

PART V — THE TRIANGULATION PRINCIPLE

  1. The Triangle as an Elementary Relational Unit

The central geometric proposition of Version III is that three distinguishable relational positions can establish an elementary triangular configuration.

A triangle contains:

  • Three vertices.
  • Three connecting edges.
  • A bounded interior region.

Within Proto-Atomos, the vertices represent distinguishable quality states or relational positions.

The edges represent defined relationships between those positions.

The interior represents the bounded configuration established by their relationship.

The triangle is therefore not merely an illustration. It is a proposed elementary unit for organizing relational information.

Three qualities can be triangulated to establish a minimal two-dimensional relational cell.

The resulting cell can be connected to other cells through shared vertices or edges.

A subject may consequently be represented through a collection of triangular configurations rather than through one isolated triangle.

  1. Why Triangulation Matters

A single quality describes an attribute.

A pair of qualities establishes a relationship.

A third distinguishable position allows those relationships to form a closed triangular configuration.

This provides a minimal geometric structure in which multiple relationships can be examined simultaneously.

For example, a substance might be examined through:

  • Structural integrity.
  • Flexibility.
  • Density.

These qualities form a triangular descriptive configuration.

The edges can represent the defined relationships between each pair.

The interior represents their combined relational configuration within the selected model.

The triangle does not prove that the three qualities exhaust the substance's identity. It establishes one bounded descriptive unit.

Additional qualities can produce additional triangles.

Additional triangles can be connected into larger structures.

  1. The Δ-Boundary and Internal Triangulation

The elementary Proto-Atomos geometry contains two different levels of triangular construction.

The first is the Δ-boundary.

This is the outer triangular enclosure representing the bounded identity-reference of the configuration.

The second is the internal triangulation.

This consists of the quality-state vertices and the triangles formed through their relationships.

The observing agent is represented within the Δ-boundary.

The internal quality-state geometry describes the subject under examination.

This distinction prevents the external boundary from being confused with the individual qualities inside it.

The outer triangle defines the representational enclosure.

The internal triangles describe the relational structure.

The complete construction is therefore a bounded geometric system containing an observer and an extensible network of quality relationships.

PART VI — THE TRIANGULATED MANIFOLD

  1. From Triangle to Manifold

The Proto-Atomos structure is not limited to one triangle.

Multiple triangular configurations may be joined through shared vertices, edges, or defined relationships.

The resulting construction is a triangulated complex.

Where the necessary geometric and local consistency conditions are satisfied, it may be treated as a triangulated manifold.

This distinction matters mathematically: a collection of connected triangles does not automatically constitute a manifold.

A valid manifold representation must satisfy additional structural conditions concerning how its local neighborhoods fit together.

Proto-Atomos therefore proposes a progressive construction:

One quality-state pair establishes a distinguishable attribute.

Three relational positions may establish a triangular cell.

Multiple cells may establish a triangulated complex.

A suitably connected and locally consistent complex may establish a manifold representation.

The complete structure can then describe a subject through its qualities and their relationships.

  1. The Miniature Manifold Principle

Each triangle may be understood as a miniature relational manifold within the larger configuration.

It represents a bounded set of relationships among distinguishable qualities.

When multiple triangles are joined, the resulting structure can express a more extensive description.

The subject becomes representable through a network of interconnected relational cells.

The resulting structure can be examined at different levels:

  • Individual quality.
  • Quality-state pair.
  • Relational edge.
  • Triangular cell.
  • Connected group of cells.
  • Complete triangulated complex.
  • Larger manifold representation.

Each level preserves information from the preceding level while introducing additional relationships.

The result is a hierarchical geometric description.

  1. Recursive Construction

The triangulated structure may be recursively subdivided.

An existing triangle may be divided into smaller triangles.

The smaller triangles may contain additional quality distinctions, measurements, or relationships.

Each subdivision increases representational resolution.

A large-scale property may therefore be examined through increasingly detailed configurations.

For example, a structural quality may be represented by one triangle at a high level.

That triangle may be subdivided into smaller relational configurations describing its components.

Those components may themselves be subdivided.

The process may continue for as long as additional meaningful information can be distinguished.

This is not a claim that every subject possesses infinite internal divisions.

It is a proposal that the representational structure can support recursive refinement.

PART VII — THE SIERPIŃSKI AND FRACTAL PRINCIPLE

  1. Recursive Triangular Geometry

The Sierpiński triangle provides a useful geometric reference for the recursive principle.

It demonstrates how a triangular configuration can be repeatedly subdivided to produce a self-similar structure.

Proto-Atomos proposes using this type of recursive triangular organization as one possible model for the expansion of relational information.

A larger triangular cell may contain smaller triangular cells.

Those smaller cells may contain still smaller cells.

The resulting construction can represent relationships at multiple scales.

This provides a geometric analogy for the organization of complex objects, concepts, and systems.

  1. Fractal Representation Does Not Imply Fractal Reality

The use of Sierpiński-like geometry is a representational proposal.

It does not establish that every physical object, psychological construct, or abstract concept is intrinsically fractal.

Nor does a fractal representation automatically constitute a conventional manifold.

A fractal complex and a manifold have different mathematical properties.

Proto-Atomos may use recursive triangulation as an organizational technique while separately determining whether the resulting structure satisfies manifold conditions.

The important principle is the ability to represent a subject at multiple levels of resolution without losing the relationships between those levels.

PART VIII — POLARITY AS PENDULATION

  1. The Pendulation Principle

Polarity is not necessarily a fixed position.

A quality may change its position along a polarity axis as the subject changes state.

Let:

Pᵢ(t)

represent the polarity position of quality i at time t.

Then:

0 ≤ Pᵢ(t) ≤ 1

A quality may move toward either Monomaxum endpoint.

It may approach the midpoint.

It may remain stable.

It may reverse direction.

It may oscillate between reference conditions.

This movement is designated as pendulation.

Pendulation describes a change in polarity position through a defined state sequence.

It does not require a perfectly periodic oscillation.

It may be continuous, discrete, irregular, cyclic, or event-driven.

  1. Magnitude and Polarity Can Change Independently

Magnitude may change without polarity changing.

Polarity may change without magnitude changing.

Both may change together.

Neither may change.

This produces four basic possibilities:

  1. Stable magnitude and stable polarity.
  2. Changing magnitude and stable polarity.
  3. Stable magnitude and changing polarity.
  4. Changing magnitude and changing polarity.

The distinction is important because a subject can preserve the degree of a quality while changing its relational direction.

Likewise, a subject can preserve its polarity position while increasing or decreasing the magnitude of that quality.

A complete state description must preserve both dimensions.

  1. Pendulation Through the Manifold

The movement of a quality through its polarity coordinate may also be represented geometrically.

A change in one quality may alter its relationship with other qualities.

That alteration may change the shape, position, or connectivity of the triangulated configuration.

The resulting state transition can be represented as a trajectory through the larger relational structure.

A sequence of states may be written:

V(t₀) → V(t₁) → V(t₂) → ... → V(tₙ)

Each state represents a configuration of quality magnitudes and polarities.

The transitions describe changes between configurations.

This allows Proto-Atomos to represent not only what a subject is described as being, but also how its description changes over time.

  1. The Folded-Manifold Hypothesis

The user-facing conceptual model proposes that polarity is a pendulation occurring within a folding of the manifold.

This suggests that qualities do not necessarily move through one simple, flat, independent coordinate system.

Their apparent positions and relationships may depend upon the larger configuration in which they are embedded.

A change in one dimension may alter the relationships between several others.

The manifold may therefore be represented as folded, layered, or multidimensional.

For Version III, this is a geometric hypothesis about the organization of state relationships.

Its possible connection to physical spacetime is a further hypothesis and is not established merely by constructing the mathematical representation.

The distinction between abstract state geometry and physical spacetime must remain explicit.

PART IX — THE MONOMAXUM REFERENCE PRINCIPLE

  1. The Two Limiting Conditions

Every dual-polarity axis requires two defined reference conditions.

These are the Monomaxum points of that axis.

They establish the limiting positions of the normalized polarity coordinate.

Pᵢ = 0

Pᵢ = 1

The points define the reference interval.

All intermediate polarity positions are described relative to those endpoints.

The Monomaxum points are not necessarily absolute conditions in the universe.

They are reference limits within a particular defined model.

Their validity depends upon the quality being represented and the consistency of the definitions.

  1. The Meaning of Zero and One

Zero and one must not be treated as universal meanings independent of context.

For one quality, zero may mean absence.

For another, it may mean minimum expression.

For another, it may mean one of two opposing conditions.

Likewise, one may represent a maximum, an opposing reference condition, or a specified terminal state.

The model must define the meaning of each endpoint before calculating intermediate positions.

This prevents a numerical interval from being mistaken for a universal ontology.

  1. Beyond Two Monomaxum Points

A quality may require more than two reference conditions.

In that case, the model may establish multiple axes, multiple coordinates, or a more complex state space.

The dual-polarity model remains the elementary case.

The general principle is that a quality must have a defined reference structure before its position can be measured or compared.

PART X — THE OBSERVING AGENT AND INFORMATIONAL POSITION

  1. Observation as an Internal State

The observing agent exists within the Δ-bounded representational configuration.

The agent possesses its own state, available information, limitations, and relationships to the subject.

The observer may be represented through a separate observer-state vector:

O = [o₁, o₂, o₃, ... oₘ]

The observer's qualities need not be identical to those of the subject.

The observer-state vector may describe:

  • Position within the model.
  • Available observations.
  • Measurement capability.
  • Informational access.
  • Interpretive assumptions.
  • Temporal position.
  • Known uncertainties.

This permits the representation to distinguish the subject's modeled state from the observer's available description of that state.

  1. Observational Displacement

The observer may encounter a subject from different positions or through different information channels.

Consequently, two observers may produce different descriptions of the same subject.

Those descriptions may differ because of:

  • Different available information.
  • Different measurement resolution.
  • Different reference conditions.
  • Different observation times.
  • Different interpretive frameworks.
  • Different positions within the relational system.

Proto-Atomos can represent these differences without automatically treating either description as the complete subject.

The model must distinguish the subject's state from the observer's reconstruction of that state.

  1. The Observer-Subject Relationship

The observer may also be a component of the subject's larger system.

For example, an organism observing its environment is itself part of the environment being modeled.

A machine monitoring itself is both an observing system and an observed system.

A conceptual system examining its own assumptions may include its own observer-state as a component.

This produces a recursive observation problem.

Proto-Atomos proposes that the observer can be included in the same representational architecture without being equated with the subject.

The observer is a distinguishable component within the larger configuration.

PART XI — THE COMPLETE GEOMETRIC IDENTITY

  1. The Proto-Atomos Configuration

A complete Version III representation contains the following elements:

  1. Δ identity: the primitive identity-reference.
  2. Δ-position: the reference position of the configuration.
  3. Δ-boundary: the outer triangular enclosure.
  4. Observer: the observing agent within the boundary.
  5. Subject: the object, concept, substance, or system being represented.
  6. Qualities: distinguishable attributes of the subject.
  7. Magnitudes: scalar measures assigned to those qualities.
  8. Polarities: normalized positions between defined reference conditions.
  9. Quality-state pairs: combined magnitude-polarity descriptions.
  10. Vectors: organized collections of quality-state pairs.
  11. Relational edges: defined connections between qualities.
  12. Triangles: elementary relational cells.
  13. Triangulated complex: a connected collection of triangular cells.
  14. Manifold representation: a suitably consistent geometric structure.
  15. Recursive subdivisions: increasingly detailed representational layers.
  16. Pendulation trajectories: changes in quality states over time.
  17. Observation records: information available to the observer.
  18. Reconstruction rules: procedures for reproducing the configuration.

The complete structure is a bounded, observer-inclusive, recursively extensible geometric representation.

  1. The Unified State Description

The minimum state description for a dual-polarity quality is:

Qᵢ = (Mᵢ, Pᵢ)

The collection of qualities is:

V = [Q₁, Q₂, ... Qₙ]

The relationship system is:

R = [R₁₂, R₁₃, ... Rᵢⱼ]

The observer is:

O = [o₁, o₂, ... oₘ]

The complete configuration can be represented conceptually as:

PA = (Δ, BΔ, O, V, R, T, H)

Where:

PA = Proto-Atomos configuration.

Δ = primitive identity-reference.

BΔ = Δ-bounded geometric configuration.

O = observer-state.

V = quality-state vector collection.

R = relational structure.

T = triangulation and connectivity.

H = history of state transitions and reconstruction information.

This expression is a proposed organizational schema. It does not imply that every component is a scalar or that all components share the same mathematical type.

Each component retains its own mathematical definition.

PART XII — OPERATIONAL USE

  1. Constructing a Proto-Atomos Representation

The device can be applied through the following procedure.

Step 1 — Identify the subject

Define what is being represented.

The subject may be physical, conceptual, mathematical, biological, mechanical, psychological, or informational.

Step 2 — Establish the Δ-reference

Establish the identity-reference and the scope of the configuration.

Define what belongs within the representational boundary and what does not.

Step 3 — Establish the observer

Identify the observing agent, its position, available information, and limitations.

Step 4 — Identify distinguishable qualities

Extract the qualities necessary to describe the subject.

Do not assume that the first collection is complete.

Step 5 — Define magnitude

For each measurable quality, establish a magnitude definition and reference interval.

Normalize to zero through one when that normalization is appropriate.

Step 6 — Define polarity

Determine whether the quality possesses a meaningful dual-polarity relationship.

Define both Monomaxum reference conditions.

Assign an intermediate coordinate where applicable.

Do not force an unsuitable quality into a dual-polarity model.

Step 7 — Construct quality-state pairs

Represent each applicable quality as:

Qᵢ = (Mᵢ, Pᵢ)

Step 8 — Construct the vector state

Collect the quality-state pairs into an organized vector-state representation.

Step 9 — Identify relationships

Determine which qualities are related and how.

Distinguish correlation, dependency, opposition, and causation.

Step 10 — Triangulate

Construct triangular relational cells from selected groups of three distinguishable positions.

Define the meaning of each vertex, edge, and interior.

Step 11 — Connect the triangles

Join compatible triangular cells into a larger triangulated complex.

Preserve shared vertices and relationships.

Step 12 — Establish the manifold representation

Determine whether the resulting complex satisfies the conditions necessary to be treated as a manifold.

If it does not, retain the more general description of a triangulated complex.

Step 13 — Examine pendulation

Observe how magnitudes, polarities, and relationships change over time.

Record the resulting state transitions.

Step 14 — Recursively refine

Subdivide areas where additional resolution is useful.

Do not increase complexity without a corresponding informational benefit.

Step 15 — Generate and examine conjectures

Use the completed representation to identify patterns, dependencies, contradictions, possible mechanisms, and unexplored relationships.

Step 16 — Verify and reconstruct

Compare the proposed representation with available evidence.

Record assumptions, uncertainties, counterexamples, and revisions.

Ensure that the construction can be reproduced from its definitions and operations.

PART XIII — MATHEMATICAL RELIABILITY AND CONSTRAINTS

  1. Definitions Must Precede Measurement

A magnitude has no reliable meaning without a defined measurement procedure.

A polarity has no reliable meaning without defined endpoints.

A triangle has no reliable meaning without defined vertices and relationships.

A manifold has no reliable meaning without the necessary geometric conditions.

Proto-Atomos must therefore treat definitions as part of the mathematical construction rather than as optional explanatory material.

  1. Normalization Must Preserve Meaning

The interval [0,1] provides a convenient common representation.

However, normalization must not destroy important differences between measurements.

Two qualities can both have a magnitude of 0.8 while representing entirely different quantities.

Their numerical equality does not establish physical, conceptual, or causal equivalence.

Every normalized quantity must retain its original definition and measurement provenance.

  1. Polarity Must Not Be Confused With Probability

A polarity coordinate between zero and one resembles a probability interval numerically.

The resemblance does not make the two equivalent.

A polarity coordinate expresses position between defined reference conditions.

A probability expresses uncertainty or likelihood under a specified probabilistic model.

A polarity value of 0.8 does not automatically mean an 80% probability.

A separate probabilistic interpretation requires separate justification.

  1. Relationships Must Be Explicit

The geometric proximity of two qualities does not establish a relationship.

The presence of a connecting edge must correspond to an explicitly defined relation.

The type and meaning of each edge should be recorded.

An edge may represent association, influence, dependency, opposition, constraint, or another relationship.

The geometric representation must not silently turn visual adjacency into a claim about causality.

  1. Triangulation Must Be Justified

Not every arbitrary collection of three qualities produces a useful triangle.

The selected qualities must have a defensible relational interpretation.

A triangle may be mathematically valid while being conceptually meaningless.

Proto-Atomos must therefore distinguish geometric validity from representational validity.

  1. Manifold Conditions Must Be Checked

A connected triangular network is not automatically a manifold.

The construction must be checked for appropriate local structure, including how cells meet at shared edges and vertices.

Where these conditions fail, the model should retain the designation of triangulated complex rather than claim a manifold without justification.

  1. Physical Interpretations Require Independent Evidence

The proposed folding, pendulation, and recursive geometry may be useful for describing abstract state relationships.

They do not, by themselves, demonstrate that physical spacetime is a Sierpiński structure, that physical qualities literally oscillate between metaphysical endpoints, or that the proposed representation is a fundamental physical field.

Such interpretations remain additional hypotheses.

The geometric model must be testable independently of its metaphysical interpretation.

PART XIV — THE GREATER PURPOSE OF PROTO-ATOMOS

  1. A Common Representation for Different Domains

Proto-Atomos proposes that diverse subjects can be represented through a shared organizational structure.

This could allow a researcher to compare otherwise dissimilar systems by examining their quality states and relationships.

For example:

A biological system may be described through physiological qualities.

A mechanical system may be described through structural and operational qualities.

An informational system may be described through encoding, retrieval, and transformation qualities.

A conceptual system may be described through definitions, assumptions, dependencies, and inferential relationships.

Their subject-specific meanings remain different.

Their representations can nevertheless share a common architecture.

  1. Conjecture Generation

Once a subject has been represented through a triangulated quality-state structure, the resulting geometry can be examined for patterns that may not be apparent through ordinary linear description.

Potential applications include:

  • Identifying relationships between qualities.
  • Detecting missing variables.
  • Comparing multiple configurations.
  • Identifying contradictory state descriptions.
  • Exploring alternative interpretations.
  • Finding possible hidden dependencies.
  • Examining changes across time.
  • Generating hypotheses from structural patterns.
  • Locating regions where information is insufficient.
  • Constructing alternative models of the same subject.

The system does not automatically establish the truth of a conjecture.

It provides a structured environment in which conjectures can be generated and examined.

  1. Orthogonal Investigation

A particularly important application is the ability to change the representational framework when a problem cannot be resolved within its original framework.

A subject may appear contradictory when represented through one set of qualities.

An alternative triangulation may reveal that the apparent contradiction arises from an incomplete description, an unsuitable polarity axis, a missing relationship, or an incorrect reference condition.

Proto-Atomos therefore permits the same subject to be reconstructed through alternative configurations.

This is an orthogonal investigation method.

Instead of repeatedly applying the same operations to an insufficient representation, the investigator changes the dimensions through which the subject is described.

The purpose is not to evade contradiction.

It is to determine whether the contradiction belongs to the subject, the model, the observer, or the representational framework.

  1. The Proto-Atomos Geometric Conjecture

We propose that any sufficiently distinguishable subject may be represented as a bounded, observer-inclusive, recursively extensible geometric configuration composed of qualities, scalar magnitudes, conceptual polarities, and defined relationships.

We further propose that each quality possessing an appropriate dual-polarity structure may be represented through a normalized polarity coordinate between two defined Monomaxum reference conditions, independently of its magnitude.

These magnitude-polarity pairs may be organized into vector-state collections, and selected groups of distinguishable quality states may be triangulated into elementary relational cells.

Multiple cells may be connected into a larger triangulated complex, and where the required geometric conditions are satisfied, a manifold representation may be established.

The resulting structure may be recursively subdivided to represent increasingly detailed relationships and may be examined through state transitions in which magnitude, polarity, and connectivity change over time.

The observing agent may be represented within the Δ-bounded configuration, permitting the subject, its qualities, and the observer's informational position to be examined within one coherent representational architecture without collapsing their distinctions.

The resulting construction is proposed as a general-purpose instrument for the organization, comparison, reconstruction, and investigation of complex subjects, and as a potential foundation for a systematic method of conjecture generation.

# PROTO-ATOMOS

The Primitive Unit of Distinction and the Geometric Assembly of Quality

MAKURABA

Abstract

This work defines a single discrete representational primitive, the proto-atomos, and constructs from it a general system for describing and relating any distinguishable subject. A proto-atomos is the bounded line of distinction between the infinitesimal and the maximal expression of one quality, together with a determinate position on that line. Part I sets out the unit: its limiting conditions, axioms, measurement standard, composition rules, layering, and worked examples. Part II joins the unit to the wider framework: Zero (the homogeneous reference condition), Delta (the relational distinction), Mono-Maximum (closure of attainable configurations), and the χ-Reentrant Closure Calculus. It then specifies comparison, temporal change, the observer, and a self-establishing descriptor network built on the primitive. The central claim is that one primitive, applied recursively, suffices to relate every describable thing in a single scale-independent geometry of quality.

# PART I — THE UNIT

1. Conventions and Notation

The following notation is used throughout.

  • q is a quality: a named, distinguishable attribute.
  • L_q is the line of distinction of q: the closed interval [0, 1] together with the definitions of its two endpoints.
  • p is position on L_q, with p ∈ [0, 1].
  • s = 2p − 1 is the signed position, with s ∈ [−1, +1].
  • k is resolution: the number of distinguishable steps on a line at a given observation.
  • ρ is the reference: the observer, the conditions, and the provenance of a value.
  • n is the layer index, with n = 0, 1, 2, …
  • ⊥ means unknown. It is never equal to 0.

Three conventions apply everywhere:

  1. Every value is meaningless without its endpoint definitions.
  2. An unknown is recorded as ⊥, never as 0. Zero asserts isn't; ⊥ asserts nothing.
  3. Numerical equality across different qualities implies no equivalence of meaning.

2. The Limiting Conditions

Definition 2.1 (Zero). Zero, M₀, is the homogeneous reference condition: a domain in which no point, region, or orientation is privileged under its symmetry group. That is, the group acts transitively. Zero is not absence. It is undifferentiated potential, the condition prior to any distinction.

Definition 2.2 (Mono-Maximum). Let C₀ be an initial configuration space and T the permitted transformations (distinction, polarity exchange, re-entry). Mono-Maximum is

M = Closure(C₀, T),

the smallest space containing C₀ and closed under T. It is maximal relative to its rules.

Principle 2.3 (Local reproduction of the limits). Every line of distinction reproduces the global limits locally. Position 0 on L_q is the Zero of quality q. Position 1 on L_q is the Mono-Maximum of quality q. The same structure therefore recurs at every scale, which is the basis of scale independence (§7).

3. The Proto-Atomos

Definition 3.1 (Line of distinction). For a quality q, the line of distinction L_q is the interval [0, 1] with:

  • 0 := the infinitesimal expression of q (isn't), and
  • 1 := the maximal expression of q (is).

Both endpoints must be stated in words before any position is assigned.

Definition 3.2 (Proto-atomos). A proto-atomos is the ordered quadruple

π = ⟨q, L_q, p, ρ⟩

That is: a quality, its line of distinction, a position on that line, and the reference under which the position was established.

Definition 3.3 (Self-reference). The proto-atomos is self-referential. Re-applying the distinction that defines it returns the same distinction:

Δ = Δ.

This is what makes it a unit rather than an event. It holds because returning to it yields it again.

Axioms of the unit

  • PA1 (Identity). Every proto-atomos satisfies Δ = Δ under its own reference ρ.
  • PA2 (Bipolarity). Every line of distinction has exactly two limits, isn't and is. A quality needing more reference conditions is represented by several proto-atomos, not by one line with extra poles.
  • PA3 (Boundedness). Every position satisfies 0 ≤ p ≤ 1. Unbounded intensities are brought into the interval by the standard of §4.
  • PA4 (Discreteness). Every observation has finite resolution k. Observed positions lie on the lattice {0, 1/k, 2/k, …, 1}. A proto-atomos is a discrete unit even when the quality it describes is continuous.
  • PA5 (Relationality). No proto-atomos exists without a reference ρ. Every distinction is made from somewhere.
  • PA6 (Scale independence). The definition and axioms of the unit are identical at every layer n.

4. Measurement Standard

S1 (Endpoint specification). State in words what p = 0 and p = 1 mean for the quality, and the procedure by which a position is obtained.

S2 (Compactification of unbounded quantities). For an intensity x ∈ [0, ∞), use

p = φ(x) = x / (x + x₀),

where x₀ > 0 is the declared reference scale at which p = 0.5. This map has four properties:

  • φ(0) = 0;
  • p → 1 as x → ∞;
  • it is strictly increasing;
  • it is invertible: x = x₀ · p / (1 − p).

So the maximal limit 1 is the ideal image of infinite magnitude, approached but not reached by any finite value.

S3 (Resolution). State k. Positions are reported as multiples of 1/k. Two positions closer than 1/k cannot be distinguished at that observation.

S4 (Provenance). Record in ρ who or what observed, when, under what conditions, and by what method.

S5 (Status). Mark each value as one of: observed, measured, inferred, proposed, or unknown (⊥).

S6 (Polarity is not probability). p = 0.8 is a position between two limits. It is not an 80% likelihood.

5. Composition: Compound Qualities and Layers

5.1 Juxtaposition (the descriptor vector)

Definition 5.1. A subject is described at layer n by the set of its proto-atomos:

D(subject) = { π₁, π₂, …, π_m }.

When written as a vector of positions, P = (p₁, …, p_m), each component keeps its own line definition. Juxtaposition places qualities side by side and does not combine them.

5.2 The compound proto-atomos

Definition 5.2. A compound proto-atomos is a proto-atomos whose quality is defined from other proto-atomos:

π_c = ⟨c, L_c, f(p₁, …, p_m), ρ⟩.

Here f : [0,1]^m → [0,1] is a declared aggregation rule satisfying:

  • (F1) Boundary agreement: f(0, …, 0) = 0 and f(1, …, 1) = 1.
  • (F2) Monotonicity: raising any input never lowers the output.

Standard aggregation rules. One of these must be named whenever a compound is formed:

  • Weighted mean: f = Σ wᵢ pᵢ, with Σ wᵢ = 1. Use when the qualities can substitute for each other.
  • Geometric mean: f = Π pᵢ^wᵢ. Use when every component is required, so that any input at 0 forces the compound to 0.
  • Minimum: f = min(pᵢ). Use for strict conjunction.

Proposition 5.3 (Closure of compounds). A compound proto-atomos satisfies PA1–PA6.

Proof. By F1, the output lies in [0, 1] and its limits coincide with those of its inputs, so PA2 and PA3 hold. Its reference is the union of its inputs' references, so PA5 holds. Under any of the standard rules, its resolution is bounded by its inputs' resolutions, so PA4 holds. The definition does not depend on n, so PA6 holds. ∎

So a compound is again a proto-atomos. This is what permits layering.

5.3 Layers

Definition 5.4. Layer 0 consists of proto-atomos measured directly. Layer n + 1 consists of proto-atomos composed from layer n, by aggregation (§5.2) or by lifting (§7). A complete description is the stack of all layers down to directly measured units.

6. Deviation and the Triangle

Proposition 6.1 (Minimality of three). A single line of distinction admits motion only toward one of its two limits. A third point not on the line, a deviation, gives the smallest closed figure with an interior: the triangle. Three is therefore the minimum number of points for a geometric structure. The triangle is also the only polygon that cannot change shape without changing its side lengths.

Definition 6.2 (Deviation decomposition). Let an existing quality axis be the unit vector u, and let a new quality have unit direction v at angle θ to u. Then:

polarity component = cos θ = ⟨u, v⟩,
deviation component = sin θ = ‖v − ⟨u, v⟩u‖.

Definition 6.3 (Residual deviation). For an existing set of orthonormal axes {u₁, …, u_r}, the residual of a new direction v is

r(v) = v − Σ ⟨uᵢ, v⟩uᵢ.

This is the Gram–Schmidt step.

  • If ‖r(v)‖ ≥ ε, the quality opens a new axis u_{r+1} = r(v)/‖r(v)‖ and a new triangular cell.
  • If ‖r(v)‖ < ε, the quality is redundant at tolerance ε and adds no new axis.

Definition 6.4 (Descriptive completeness). A subject is completely described at tolerance ε when no further candidate quality has residual deviation ≥ ε. This is the stopping rule for description.

Principle 6.5 (Qualities are relational). A quality is located in the triangle, not at a vertex. Example 8.2 shows this explicitly.

7. Lifting and Scale Independence

Definition 7.1 (Closed configuration). A set of proto-atomos with their relations is closed when it is complete at tolerance ε (Definition 6.4) and stable under re-observation (PA1).

Definition 7.2 (Lifting). The lifting map Λ sends a closed configuration K at layer n to a single proto-atomos at layer n + 1:

Λ(K) = ⟨q_K, L_K, p_K, ρ_K⟩,

where q_K names the configuration as one quality, L_K defines its limits, and p_K is given by a declared aggregation rule over K.

Proposition 7.3 (Scale independence). For every n, the units at layer n satisfy the same axioms as units at layer 0.

Proof. Lifting produces a proto-atomos by Definition 3.2, and Proposition 5.3 applies to its aggregation. ∎

Consequently one primitive serves for a quality, an object, a concept, a system, or a world. Only the layer index changes.

8. Worked Examples

8.1 A descriptor vector (layer 0)

Subject: a dog. Reference ρ: one observer, daylight, visual inspection. Resolution k = 100.

  • Fur — 0 := hairless; 1 := maximally dense coat. p = 0.90.
  • Brown — 0 := no brown hue; 1 := fully brown. p = 0.67.
  • Red — 0 := no red hue; 1 := fully red. p = 0.54.

Signed form: s = (0.80, 0.34, 0.08). Brown and red are separate lines. Their values are independent and are not complementary, since both can be high at once.

8.2 A relational quality: wetness

Wetness is fixed by three interfacial tensions, solid–vapor, solid–liquid and liquid–vapor (Young's relation: γ_SV = γ_SL + γ_LV cos θ). It is therefore a triangle by construction.

Define L_wet with 0 := complete non-wetting (θ = 180°) and 1 := complete spreading (θ = 0°), so that p = (1 + cos θ) / 2.

  • Mercury on glass, θ ≈ 140°: p ≈ (1 − 0.766)/2 ≈ 0.12.
  • Mercury on clean gold, which it wets and amalgamates with, θ → 0°: p → 1.
  • Water on clean glass, θ ≈ 0–20°: p ≈ 0.97–1.00.

The liquid is the same and the wetness is opposite. The quality belongs to the configuration (liquid, surface, medium), not to the liquid alone. On gold, mercury is "wet" in exactly the sense that water is "wet" on glass: both occupy the same region of L_wet.

8.3 Deviation

Let the existing axis be u = (1, 0) and a new quality v at θ = 60°.

  • Polarity component: cos 60° = 0.500.
  • Deviation component: sin 60° ≈ 0.866.

With ε = 0.1, ‖r(v)‖ ≈ 0.866 ≥ ε, so v opens a new axis and a triangular cell. At θ = 3°, the deviation is sin 3° ≈ 0.052 < ε, so the quality is redundant at this tolerance.

8.4 A compound and a lift

At layer 0, take three proto-atomos of the dog: fur p = 0.90, self-motion p = 0.95, body warmth p = 0.85.

Define the compound animate-mammalian by the geometric mean with equal weights:

p = (0.90 × 0.95 × 0.85)^(1/3) ≈ 0.90.

If the configuration is closed at tolerance ε, Λ lifts it to a single layer-1 proto-atomos, mammal, p ≈ 0.90, which can now be juxtaposed with other layer-1 units such as domesticated or quadruped. A stuffed toy dog yields self-motion p ≈ 0. Under the geometric mean the compound collapses to 0, which correctly separates it from the animal.

# PART II — THE UNIFIED SYSTEM

9. Genesis: From Zero to Distinction

The framework forms a single generative sequence:

M₀ → Δ → π → R → D → O → … → M

That is: homogeneous Zero; the first distinction; the proto-atomos; relational organization; differentiated structure; observable organization; and, in the limit of closure, Mono-Maximum.

The step M₀ → Δ is a collapse between the two limits. The undifferentiated condition and the condition of total attainment meet in a single distinction, this and not that. Every proto-atomos is a local instance of that collapse: a line from its own Zero to its own Mono-Maximum, with a position on it.

The sequence is recursive. Any differentiated region may serve as a new homogeneous reference for further distinction:

M₀ → Δ₁ → M₁ → Δ₂ → M₂ → …

10. Correspondence with χ-RCC

The χ-Reentrant Closure Calculus is one formal lens on the system. Its operators correspond to proto-atomos operations as follows.

  • Recurrence axiom A2 (∃s, s ∈ reach(s)) corresponds to PA1, Δ = Δ.
  • Identity class [s]≡ (strongly connected component) corresponds to a stable proto-atomos.
  • D (Differentiate, 1 → 2) corresponds to establishing the two limits of a line.
  • C (Couple, 2 → 1, symmetric) corresponds to the relation that closes a triangle.
  • A (Absorb, 2 → 1, irreversible) corresponds to lifting Λ.
  • S (Suspend) corresponds to a unit retained in the record but inactive. Unknown units remain ⊥.
  • Constraint field χ and forbidden compositions correspond to the admissibility of operations on each unit.
  • Theorem 3 (observation as fixed-point selection) corresponds to the interior observer (§12).
  • Theorem 4 (structural self-similarity) corresponds to scale independence (Proposition 7.3).
  • Two fibers over one base correspond to the two limits of one quality.

The reference implementation verifies the corrected Theorems T1, T1b and T3 on randomly generated digraphs without counterexample, and enforces balance, admissibility and transactional rollback. This establishes that the operations are consistent and executable. It does not establish the philosophical claims, which stand or fall on their own terms.

11. Change: Pendulation and Ripple

Definition 11.1 (Pendulation). The time-indexed position p(t) of a proto-atomos is its pendulation. It need not be periodic. In the extended form Q = (M, P), magnitude and polarity may change independently, which gives four basic cases: both stable, magnitude changing, polarity changing, or both changing.

Definition 11.2 (Trajectory). A sequence of descriptor states D(t₀) → D(t₁) → … → D(t_n) is a trajectory through the space of configurations.

Hypothesis 11.3 (Ripple). A pendulating proto-atomos is the peak or trough of a ripple, the expression in observable reality of patterns of higher dimension. Any subject is then a superposition of such ripples, a wave packet of qualities. This hypothesis is stated separately from the representational system (see §15).

12. The Observer

Postulate 12.1 (Interior observer). The observer lies within every configuration it describes and is one vertex of every triangle it constructs. In χ-RCC this follows from Axiom A1 (closure): no observation escapes the domain.

Definition 12.2 (Delta relation). Following the Delta framework, every distinction is a relation among observer, reference point, conditions, and trajectory. These four components must be kept distinguishable.

Definition 12.3 (Observational displacement). For two observers a and b describing the same subject, the displacement on quality q is

δ_q = p_q(a) − p_q(b).

A non-zero displacement is data. It must be attributed to one of: a change in the subject, a difference in reference, a difference in resolution, or a difference in timing. It must not be averaged away.

Principle 12.4. Observation establishes distinguishability. It does not create physical reality.

13. Comparison and Cross-Domain Mapping

Definition 13.1 (Distance between descriptions). Let A and B be described on aligned qualities, meaning qualities with identical line definitions, and with weights w. Then

d(A, B) = √( Σ wᵢ (p_A,ᵢ − p_B,ᵢ)² ) + λ · |R_A Δ R_B|,

where the second term counts the relations present in one description and absent in the other. Qualities present on only one side are reported separately as vocabulary difference.

Definition 13.2 (Cross-domain map). A map φ from the lines of domain A to the lines of domain B is structure-preserving when:

  1. it sends limits to limits (0 → 0 and 1 → 1, or consistently reversed);
  2. it carries relations to relations; and
  3. it carries pendulation to pendulation.

Standard 13.3. Every comparison reports three things: what matches, what mismatches, and what cannot be compared. It must also outperform a random mapping on the same data before any correspondence is asserted.

Example 8.2 is a structure-preserving map. The line L_wet carries water–glass and mercury–gold to the same region with limits preserved.

14. Application: A Self-Establishing Descriptor Network

Definition 14.1 (Node). A node N is a set of proto-atomos D(N) whose first member is its own self-reference, π_self with Δ = Δ.

Network operations.

  1. Grounding. Every node begins with π_self.
  2. Self-description. The node assigns positions to its own qualities under its own reference.
  3. Relation. When nodes N₁ and N₂ register each other, their relation becomes a proto-atomos held in a triangle with the observing node as vertex.
  4. Learning by deviation. An observation adds structure only if its residual deviation exceeds ε (Definition 6.3).
  5. Abstraction by lifting. Closed configurations are lifted to layer n + 1 (Definition 7.2).
  6. Distributed reference. No node holds a description of the whole. The network is established through the referenced partial descriptions of all its nodes.

Property 14.2 (Legibility). Every internal state of the network is a set of named qualities with defined limits and positions. The network's reasoning is therefore readable as description rather than as opaque parameters.

Property 14.3 (Replay). If every operation is recorded, every state can be reconstructed by replaying the record from π_self. In the χ-RCC implementation the record is hash-chained.

15. Standards of Validity and Scope

Five forms of evaluation are kept distinct:

  1. Formal validity — the construction satisfies its definitions and axioms.
  2. Representational validity — the construction faithfully describes its subject.
  3. Observational support — the positions agree with observation within resolution.
  4. Inferential support — conclusions follow from definitions and premises.
  5. Empirical confirmation — independent evidence supports a physical interpretation.

The representational system (Part I and §§12–14) does not depend on Hypothesis 11.3. The claim that polarity is a ripple of higher-dimensional pattern in spacetime is a separate physical hypothesis and requires independent evidence.

16. Refutation Criteria and Open Problems

The conjecture is weakened or refuted by any of the following:

  1. A class of distinguishable subjects that cannot be described to any tolerance ε by finite stacks of proto-atomos, or that is described no better than by a simpler baseline.
  2. Structure-preserving cross-domain maps whose preservation fails to predict which transferred inferences are correct.
  3. A descriptor network with lifting that describes new subjects no more efficiently over time than one without it.
  4. A structure requiring a primitive that cannot be decomposed into lines of distinction and deviation.

Open problems include:

  • a minimal local update rule for a triangulated lattice of proto-atomos;
  • a canonical choice of aggregation rule per class of quality;
  • whether iterated lifting reaches a fixed point (χ-ergodic) or continues indefinitely (χ-creative).

17. Condensed Identity

  • Limits: M₀ (Zero), and M = Closure(C₀, T) (Mono-Maximum).
  • Primitive: π = ⟨q, L_q, p, ρ⟩, with Δ = Δ and p ∈ [0, 1].
  • Signed position: s = 2p − 1.
  • Compactification: p = x / (x + x₀).
  • Resolution: p ∈ {0, 1/k, …, 1}.
  • Compound: p_c = f(p₁, …, p_m), with f monotone and boundary-preserving.
  • Deviation: cos θ = ⟨u, v⟩; residual r(v) = v − Σ ⟨uᵢ, v⟩uᵢ.
  • Completeness: ‖r(v)‖ < ε for every remaining candidate quality.
  • Lift: Λ(K) = π at layer n + 1.
  • Scale independence: the axioms PA1–PA6 hold at every n.

18. Conclusion

Between Zero and Mono-Maximum lies the line of distinction, and on it the proto-atomos: one quality, two limits, one position, one reference. It is discrete in observation, bounded in value, and identical in form at every scale. Through compounding it forms layers. Through deviation it forms triangles. Through lifting it forms units of higher order, and the observer is always one of its vertices. The conjecture is that this single primitive is sufficient to relate all describable things in one geometry of quality, and that an intelligence can establish itself from its descriptors.

Status: proposed conjecture. Open to formalization, implementation, and refutation.
