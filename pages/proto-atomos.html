PROTO-ATOMOS

# PROTO-ATOMOS

Part One: The Primitive Point of Self-Reference

The Magnitude-Scalar, Polarity and Recursive Triangulated Conjecture, and the Unitary Instrument of Abstraction

MAKURABA · Complete revised edition

Abstract

Proto-Atomos is proposed as a unitary instrument of abstraction. It is a single representational primitive, together with the rules for using it, through which an operational agent can identify, describe, compare, transform and reconstruct any distinguishable subject: object, substance, organism, process, concept, mind or system. It does so through the subject's qualities and relationships, without first needing the subject's native descriptive language.

The primitive, the proto-atomos, is the first self-referential distinction between is and isn't. It is a bounded line of distinction between the infinitesimal and maximal expression of one quality, with a determinate position on that line, established from a reference inside the system.

The primitive arises between two limiting conditions. Zero is the homogeneous condition before any distinction. Mono-Maximum is the closure of everything attainable. The primitive carries a magnitude and a polarity. Through deviation it closes the first geometric figure, the triangle. Its position can change over time (pendulation), and the observer who places it always stands inside the representation.

Built on this primitive are a quality record and a shared scripture of definitions, a discipline of synesthetic comparison across domains, and a method of orthogonal investigation for problems that resist their original framing. Mathematics is treated as one of several lenses on the primitive, not as its foundation.

This Part has two divisions. Division I states the conjecture in conceptual language. Division II constructs it formally: definitions, axioms, standards, theorems, worked examples, correspondences and limits. Part Two develops the lattice of compound objects built from many primitives.

# DIVISION I — THE CONJECTURE

1. Statement

There is a single primitive point of self-reference, and every describable structure can assemble itself geometrically from it. That point is the first distinction between is and isn't. Its substance is that polarity and nothing else.

That point is the proto-atomos.

2. Before Distinction: Zero

Before anything is distinguished there is zero. Zero is not emptiness. It is homogeneity: a continuous domain in which no point is privileged, no direction is preferred, no center is given, and no boundary exists. It is pure potential. Nothing in it is separate, so nothing in it can yet be observed.

3. The Opposite Copy: Mono-Maximum

Facing zero is its opposite copy, the Mono-Maximum: the totality of every configuration that can be reached, closed under its own transformations. Zero is the infinitesimal end, where nothing is yet distinguished. Mono-Maximum is the infinite end, where everything attainable is attained.

4. The Collapse: Δ = Δ

When the void and its opposite meet, they collapse into a distinction: this, and not that. That collapse is Δ.

Δ refers to itself. It is the first thing that can point at itself and hold: Δ = Δ. It has no content yet. It is only the fact that something is where it otherwise isn't.

That self-reference is the proto-atomos: the most primitive point of observational being. It is awareness in its smallest form, the bare registering of a difference.

The term does not mean a physical atom or a smallest material particle. It means the smallest unit of construction for a description: the point at which anything first becomes distinguishable.

5. Polarity: Is and Isn't

Every proto-atomos carries a polarity. One pole is isn't: the infinitesimal, 0. The other pole is is: the infinite, 1. These two reference conditions are the quality's Monomaxum points. They are local copies of the two great ends, Zero and Mono-Maximum.

Any concept can be placed between them. Take a dog:

  • Fur 0.9
  • Brown 0.67
  • Red 0.54

Each number says how far that quality has moved from isn't toward is. Put the qualities together and you have the dog: a readable vector of traits, tokenized the way a person would describe it rather than in a hidden code.

This is not limited to language. It is a calculus of description for anything that can be distinguished: a primitive of thought, of mathematics and of description at once.

6. Magnitude Is Not Polarity

Polarity answers where a quality stands between its two limits. Magnitude answers how much of the quality is present. A quality can be strong and close to either pole, or weak and close to either pole. The two must never be collapsed into each other.

7. Deviation: The Third Point

A polarity alone is a line, and along a line you can only move toward is or toward isn't.

The third point is deviation: movement off that line. Deviation closes the first shape, the triangle. It is the minimum needed for any geometry, and it is where a quality stops being a position on a line and becomes a form.

Read through wave trigonometry, every new point is part polarity (cos) and part deviation (sin). Each deviation opens a new axis and a new triangle. When no new deviation remains, the thing is completely described.

8. The Ripple, Pendulation and the Fold

Polarity is not static. A quality can move toward either pole, settle at the middle, reverse or oscillate. This motion is pendulation.

The conjecture holds that polarity is a ripple: the trace that higher-dimensional patterns leave as they appear in 2D and 3D reality. A proto-atomos is the peak or dip of that ripple. Anything can be described as a mass of such peaks and dips, a wave packet of qualities.

Because qualities depend on one another, pendulation does not happen on a flat, independent coordinate. It happens within a folding of the larger structure. That is why a change in one quality can bend the relations among many others.

9. Recursion

Any triangle can be subdivided into smaller triangles, each holding finer distinctions, in the manner of the Sierpiński triangle. Description can be refined as far as meaningful distinctions continue to appear, and it stops when they don't. This is a power of representation, not a claim that reality is infinitely divided.

10. The Observer

Because the proto-atomos is a point of awareness, there is no observer outside it. Every observer is made of distinctions and is one vertex of every triangle it describes. Observation is not a view from outside. It is the system distinguishing from within.

Two observers can therefore describe the same subject differently without either being wrong. A change in interpretation is not necessarily a change in the thing. A change in the thing can go unnoticed. And what is unknown is not thereby absent.

11. Synesthesia and Comparison

Different things can share a quality without being the same thing. Mercury is not wet on glass, but it is wet on gold, in exactly the sense that water is wet on glass. "Sharp" names something real in a sound, a taste, a blade and an argument.

Synesthetic abstraction is the structured carrying of a quality from one domain to another. The quality's shape is preserved, and each domain keeps its own meaning. Identity, equivalence, similarity, analogy, association, correlation and causation are different relations. None may stand in for another.

12. The Quality Scripture

Qualities are recorded in a shared scripture of definitions. Each entry states what the quality means, how it is measured, what its two poles stand for, where its values come from, and how it relates to other qualities. The scripture lets descriptions accumulate across subjects, observers and time, and it lets an agent meet something new and describe it using what it already knows.

13. Orthogonal Investigation

When a problem cannot be solved in the language it arrived in, the instrument changes the language. It selects different qualities, replaces an unsuitable axis, adds a dimension, or widens the boundary. The aim is not to escape the contradiction. It is to find out whether the contradiction belongs to the subject, the model, the observer or the framework.

14. Purpose: A Unitary Instrument for an Indefinite Agent

Proto-Atomos is meant for an agent whose domain is not known in advance: one that will meet the unfamiliar and the incomplete, and has to begin describing before it understands. The agent identifies qualities. Qualities establish distinctions. Distinctions permit comparison. Comparison reveals relationships. Relationships permit triangulation. Triangulation gives geometric organization, which permits reconstruction, transformation and further abstraction.

The deepest purpose is a descriptor system from which an intelligence can establish itself out of quality descriptors of its own self-reference. Such an intelligence would be readable all the way down, because every one of its states is a description.

15. The Lenses

Mathematics is not the foundation of this conjecture. It is one lens. Wave trigonometry, χ-RCC, topology and plain language are each a chart of the same structure. A concept is whatever survives translation from one lens to another, and that survival is the test of whether it is true. Infinity enters the conjecture as a horizon to be pointed toward, not a quantity to be calculated.

# DIVISION II — THE FORMAL CONSTRUCTION

16. Conventions and Notation

  • q — a quality: a named, distinguishable attribute.
  • L_q — the line of distinction of q: the interval [0, 1] together with definitions of its two endpoints.
  • p — position on L_q, with p ∈ [0, 1].
  • s = 2p − 1 — signed position, with s ∈ [−1, +1].
  • φ — phase angle of the primitive, with φ ∈ [0, π] (§24).
  • M, P — magnitude and polarity in the extended state.
  • k — resolution: the number of distinguishable steps on a line at a given observation.
  • ρ — reference: observer, conditions and provenance.
  • τ — the observer's temporal pointer.
  • ⊥ — unknown. Never equal to 0.

Three conventions hold throughout:

  1. No value has meaning without its endpoint definitions.
  2. An unknown is recorded as ⊥, never as 0. Zero asserts isn't; ⊥ asserts nothing.
  3. Numerical equality across different qualities implies no equivalence of meaning.

17. The Limiting Conditions

17.1 Zero

Definition 17.1 (Zero). Zero, written M₀, is a homogeneous reference condition: a domain in which no point, region or orientation is privileged under its symmetry group. The group acts transitively, meaning that for any two points some symmetry carries one to the other.

Five notions are kept apart:

  • Nothingness: no object or quantity is present.
  • Emptiness: a region has no specified contents.
  • Homogeneity: properties are uniform under the relevant transformations.
  • Continuity: connectedness without discrete separation.
  • Zero: the additive identity (x + 0 = x), and the reference from which departures are measured.

Zero, as used here, refers to homogeneity: undifferentiated potential, not nothing.

Four consequences follow:

  1. No privileged center. A homogeneous domain has no intrinsic center. A center, when one appears, is a relational property introduced by a reference, a boundary or an observer.
  2. Description is not constitution. Coordinates can be laid on a homogeneous domain without making any position intrinsically special.
  3. The number zero is not dimension zero. A zero-dimensional manifold is a discrete set of points. The homogeneous reference condition may be modeled in any dimension.
  4. Zero relates to infinity only through limits. 1/x tends to +∞ as x approaches 0 from above, and to −∞ as x approaches 0 from below. One reference condition supports opposite limiting behaviors depending on how it is approached.

Four categories are also kept apart: what is specified, what is not yet specified, what is mathematically permitted, and what is physically realized. Zero is potential in the sense that it does not yet select a differentiated arrangement. It does not hold every outcome as a hidden substance.

17.2 Mono-Maximum

Definition 17.2 (Mono-Maximum). Let C₀ be an initial configuration space and T a set of permitted transformations: Δ-transitions, polarity exchanges and re-entry. Mono-Maximum is

M = Closure(C₀, T) = ⋃ₙ Cₙ, where Cₙ₊₁ = Cₙ ∪ T(Cₙ).

It is the smallest space that contains C₀ and is closed under T. Its maximality is relative to its rules. It does not claim to exhaust every conceivable structure, only every structure attainable from C₀ under T.

Principle 17.3 (Local reproduction of the limits). Every line of distinction reproduces the global limits locally. Position 0 on L_q is the Zero of quality q, and position 1 is its Mono-Maximum. The same structure recurs at every scale.

18. Genesis and the Delta Relation

Definition 18.1 (Genesis sequence).

M₀ → Δ → R → D → O

That is: the homogeneous condition, the establishment of distinction, relational organization, differentiated structure, and observable organization. The sequence is conceptual, not necessarily temporal. It is recursive, since any differentiated region may serve as a new reference condition:

M₀ → Δ₁ → M₁ → Δ₂ → M₂ → …

A distinction does not destroy continuity. A connected space can contain distinguishable regions: a subset A ⊂ M is distinguished from its complement M \ A without disconnecting M. Differentiation and discontinuity are not the same thing.

Definition 18.2 (Delta). Every distinction Δ is a relation among four components:

  • observer: the agent taking part in the relation;
  • reference point: the perspective from which conditions are read;
  • conditions: the internal and external influences on the present state;
  • trajectory: the possible transformations available from the current configuration.

Position, reference and trajectory must stay distinct. A change of position need not change the reference. A change of reference can change how a position is read without changing the position. A trajectory is a possibility, not a guarantee: an anticipated trajectory must not be confused with an observed one.

Definition 18.3 (Recursive Delta cycle).

Observe → Interpret → Weigh → Act → Transform → Re-evaluate

Each stage conditions the next, and the cycle has no predetermined end.

Definition 18.4 (Conditional agency). Conditions are classified as follows:

  • presently influenceable;
  • changeable through deliberate action;
  • requiring external cooperation;
  • currently limiting;
  • wrongly assumed to be unchangeable.

Delta assumes neither absolute control nor absolute powerlessness.

Definition 18.5 (Uncertainty). Every Delta distinguishes established observations, reasonable inferences, unverified assumptions and unknown conditions. Greater accuracy does not abolish uncertainty. It lets uncertainty be located and classified.

19. The Primitive

Definition 19.1 (Line of distinction). For a quality q, L_q is the interval [0, 1] with:

  • 0 := the infinitesimal expression of q (isn't);
  • 1 := the maximal expression of q (is).

Both endpoints must be stated in words before any position is assigned.

Definition 19.2 (Proto-atomos). A proto-atomos is the ordered quadruple

π = ⟨q, L_q, p, ρ⟩

That is: a quality, its line of distinction, a position on that line, and the reference under which the position was established.

Definition 19.3 (Self-reference). A proto-atomos is self-referential: re-applying the distinction that defines it returns the same distinction,

Δ = Δ.

This is what makes it a unit rather than an event. It holds because returning to it yields it again.

Axioms of the primitive

  • PA1 (Identity). Every proto-atomos satisfies Δ = Δ under its own reference.
  • PA2 (Bipolarity). Every line of distinction has exactly two limits. A quality needing more reference conditions is represented by several primitives (§22.6).
  • PA3 (Boundedness). 0 ≤ p ≤ 1. Unbounded intensities enter by compactification (§23).
  • PA4 (Discreteness). Every observation has a finite resolution k, so observed positions lie in {0, 1/k, …, 1}. The primitive is discrete even when the quality it describes is continuous.
  • PA5 (Relationality). No proto-atomos exists without a reference. Every distinction is made from somewhere.
  • PA6 (Interiority). The reference lies inside the represented domain. No primitive is placed from outside.
  • PA7 (Scale independence). The definition and axioms of the primitive are the same at every scale of description.

20. The Quality

Definition 20.1 (Quality). A quality is any attribute, property, condition, behavior or feature that can be distinguished within an investigation. A quality need not be numerical before it is useful. It may begin as qualitative, symbolic, comparative, perceptual, relational or incompletely defined.

Examples include colour, temperature, density, rigidity, flexibility, shape, texture, sound, frequency, motion, stability, complexity, composition, function, identity, meaning, similarity, difference, accessibility, dependency, direction, state and behaviour. These belong to different mathematical kinds. Some are measured directly, some need an operational definition, some are categorical, some are inferred, and some stay uncertain.

Principle 20.2 (Differentiation before measurement). The first operation is not measurement. It is differentiation: establishing that a quality can be told apart from other qualities of the subject.

A quality can be identified through any of the following:

  • direct observation;
  • measurement;
  • comparison;
  • functional behaviour;
  • structural composition;
  • relation to another quality;
  • repeated occurrence;
  • symbolic or linguistic description;
  • inference;
  • an explicit hypothesis.

Principle 20.3 (Context retention). A quality keeps the context in which it was introduced. "Hardness" as a measured material property, as a tactile impression, and as a metaphor for a concept are not mathematically equivalent merely because they share a word.

Definition 20.4 (Quality record). Each proto-atomos is held in a record with these fields:

  • identity and name;
  • definition;
  • subject;
  • domain;
  • origin of the observation;
  • method of measurement or classification;
  • magnitude, if applicable;
  • polarity, if applicable;
  • endpoint definitions;
  • relations to other qualities;
  • confidence;
  • temporal pointer τ;
  • provenance and revision history.

An incomplete record is allowed. Unresolved attributes are kept as unresolved.

Definition 20.5 (Status). Every value carries one of these statuses: known, observed, measured, inferred, proposed, unknown (⊥), contradicted or unresolved.

Principle 20.6 (Distinguishability is not independence). Qualities that can be told apart may still depend on one another. They may be correlated, causally dependent, mutually exclusive, mutually reinforcing or suppressing, or emergent from interaction. The quality is therefore recorded separately from its relations, and relations are part of the representation, not commentary on it.

21. Magnitude

Definition 21.1 (Magnitude). M ∈ [0, 1] is the degree, extent, intensity, quantity or expression of a quality under a defined measurement. M = 0 is the defined lower or absent condition, and M = 1 is the defined upper reference condition.

Principle 21.2 (Shared coordinate, distinct meaning). A temperature intensity of 0.8, a structural integrity of 0.8 and an informational completeness of 0.8 share a coordinate, not a meaning. Normalization permits comparison of degree within a defined representation. It establishes no equivalence across qualities.

Principle 21.3 (Comparability). M₁(A) and M₁(B) may be compared only when their measurement definitions, reference conditions and normalization are compatible. Where they are not, the mismatch is reported rather than an equivalence manufactured.

Principle 21.4 (Representational latitude). Where normalization would distort meaning, a quality may keep unnormalized values, ranges or categorical values.

22. Polarity

Definition 22.1 (Monomaxum points). A polarity is fixed by two distinguishable reference conditions, its Monomaxum points: P = 0 at the first and P = 1 at the second. The points are reference limits within a defined model, not absolute conditions of the universe.

22.2 Example axes. Possible polarity axes include:

  • expansion ↔ contraction;
  • attraction ↔ repulsion;
  • flexible ↔ rigid;
  • open ↔ closed;
  • inclusion ↔ exclusion;
  • presence ↔ absence;
  • stability ↔ instability;
  • compression ↔ extension;
  • activation ↔ inhibition;
  • continuity ↔ discontinuity;
  • centralization ↔ distribution;
  • retention ↔ release.

These illustrate reference relations. They are not claims of perfect opposition. An axis may be symmetric, asymmetric, contextual or constrained.

Definition 22.3 (Signed polarity). s = 2P − 1, so P = 0 maps to s = −1, P = 0.5 to s = 0, and P = 1 to s = +1. The two forms are interchangeable when the endpoint definitions are held fixed.

Principle 22.4 (The midpoint is not neutrality). P = 0.5 is the midpoint of the interval. It does not mean equilibrium, neutrality or absence unless that is separately defined.

Principle 22.5 (Endpoints are contextual). For one quality 0 means absence; for another, minimum expression; for another, one of two opposed conditions. The same holds for 1. Endpoints are defined before intermediate positions are calculated, so that a numerical interval is never mistaken for a universal ontology.

Principle 22.6 (Polarity does not make the world binary). A subject may have many qualities, each with its own axis. Some qualities have no meaningful polarity. Others need several axes, multidimensional coordinates or categorical reference structures. A single quality may carry several independent axes at once, such as expansion–contraction, stability–instability and activation–inhibition, and these are never collapsed into one universal polarity.

Principle 22.7 (Polarity is not probability). A polarity coordinate expresses position between reference conditions. A probability expresses likelihood under a probabilistic model. P = 0.8 is not an 80% likelihood, and any probabilistic reading must be justified separately.

23. Measurement Standard

S1 — Endpoints. State in words what p = 0 and p = 1 mean, and the procedure that yields a position.

S2 — Compactification. For an unbounded intensity x ∈ [0, ∞), use

p = x / (x + x₀),

where x₀ > 0 is the declared reference scale at which p = 0.5. The map is strictly increasing and invertible: x = x₀ · p / (1 − p). The limit 1 is the ideal image of infinite magnitude. It is approached as x grows but never reached by a finite value. This is the precise sense in which the is-pole is the infinite.

S3 — Resolution. State k, and report positions as multiples of 1/k. Positions closer together than 1/k cannot be distinguished at that observation.

S4 — Provenance. Record the observer, time, conditions and method in ρ, and the temporal pointer τ.

S5 — Status. Mark every value with its status (Definition 20.5).

S6 — No probability reading. Polarity values are never read as probabilities (Principle 22.7).

24. The Phase Form: One Angle for Polarity and Deviation

Definition 24.1 (Phase of a primitive). Every position p ∈ [0, 1] corresponds to a phase angle φ ∈ [0, π] through

p = (1 + cos φ) / 2, equivalently s = cos φ.

The is-pole sits at φ = 0 and the isn't-pole at φ = π.

Proposition 24.2 (Polarity and deviation are one angle). Under the phase form, polarity is the cosine of the phase and deviation is its sine:

polarity component = cos φ = s, deviation component = sin φ = √(1 − s²).

Proof. s = cos φ by definition, and on [0, π] the sine is non-negative and equals √(1 − cos² φ). ∎

The phase form unifies Division I §§5 and 7. A primitive is a point on a semicircle. Its projection onto the axis is its polarity. Its height above the axis is its deviation, the measure of how far it has left the line. At either pole the deviation is zero: the quality is fully decided. At the midpoint the deviation is maximal: the quality is fully open to a new direction.

Definition 24.3 (Pendulation as phase motion). Pendulation is φ(t). Uniform oscillation (φ = ωt) gives the harmonic form s(t) = cos ωt, but pendulation need not be periodic. It may be continuous, discrete, irregular, cyclic or event-driven.

Principle 24.4 (Minimal observable window). In signal analysis, the smallest resolvable packet of a wave is called an atom, and its spreads in time and frequency obey the Gabor limit

σ_t · σ_f ≥ 1/(4π).

The conjecture adopts this as the model of the primitive's observability. The resolution k of a primitive is bounded by the observer's window: no observer can sharpen a quality's position and its rate of change without limit at the same time. The proto-atomos is the minimal observable window of a quality.

25. The Quality State and Vector Traits

Definition 25.1 (Extended state). A quality with both a defined magnitude and a defined polarity is written

Q = (M, P), with M ≠ P.

For example, Q = (0.80, 0.20) is a quality of magnitude 0.80 and polarity 0.20. It does not mean "80% positive," "20% present" or "80% probable."

Definition 25.2 (Vector traits). Where they are meaningfully defined, a quality may also carry:

  • a direction;
  • a rate of change;
  • a temporal state;
  • a relational influence;
  • a contextual dependency.

These are kept as separate coordinates, never collapsed into one scalar. Magnitude can hold steady while the direction of change reverses, and polarity can hold steady while magnitude grows.

Definition 25.3 (Descriptor). A subject is described by its set of primitives,

D(subject) = { π₁, …, π_m },

each keeping its own line definition. Composition of primitives into compound qualities, layers and objects is the subject of Part Two.

Principle 25.4 (Change cases). Magnitude and polarity can change independently, giving four basic cases:

  1. both stable;
  2. magnitude changing, polarity stable;
  3. polarity changing, magnitude stable;
  4. both changing.

A complete state description preserves all four.

26. Deviation and the Triangle

Proposition 26.1 (Minimality of three). A single line of distinction allows motion only toward one of its two limits. A third point off the line, a deviation, closes the smallest figure with an interior: the triangle. Three is the minimum number of points for any geometric structure. The triangle is also the only polygon that is rigid, meaning it cannot change shape without changing its side lengths.

Three points are what turn a relation into a structure:

  • one quality establishes an attribute;
  • two establish a comparison;
  • three close a configuration in which several relations can be examined at once.

Definition 26.2 (Deviation decomposition). For an existing unit axis u and a new unit direction v at angle θ:

polarity component = cos θ = ⟨u, v⟩, deviation component = sin θ = ‖v − ⟨u, v⟩ u‖.

Definition 26.3 (Residual deviation). For orthonormal axes {u₁, …, u_r}, the residual of v is

r(v) = v − Σ ⟨uᵢ, v⟩ uᵢ,

which is one Gram–Schmidt step. If ‖r(v)‖ ≥ ε, the quality opens a new axis, u_{r+1} = r(v) / ‖r(v)‖. If ‖r(v)‖ < ε, the quality is redundant at tolerance ε.

Definition 26.4 (Descriptive completeness). A subject is completely described at tolerance ε when no remaining candidate quality has residual deviation of at least ε. This is the stopping rule for description and for refinement.

Definition 26.5 (Meaning of the triangle). In a triangle of three primitives:

  • each vertex is a quality state;
  • each edge is a typed relation between two qualities: association, dependency, opposition, influence, equivalence or constraint;
  • the interior is the space of joint configurations of the three.

A point in the interior is given by barycentric coordinates

(λ₁, λ₂, λ₃), with λᵢ ≥ 0 and λ₁ + λ₂ + λ₃ = 1,

which state how much each vertex contributes to a combined condition. The interior therefore has a definite meaning: it is the set of all weighted combinations of the three quality states. It is not a new physical property unless one is separately established.

Principle 26.6 (Triangles must be justified). Not every set of three qualities makes a meaningful triangle. A triangle can be geometrically valid and conceptually empty. Its selection must rest on a defensible relation among its vertices, such as three mutually related qualities, three dimensions of one property, three components of a function, three conditions of a process, three corresponding properties across domains, or three observational positions.

Principle 26.7 (Qualities are relational). A quality lives in the triangle, not at a vertex (Example 30.2). The relations are part of the subject's representation, not added commentary.

Definition 26.8 (Δ-boundary and internal triangulation). The elementary geometry has two levels:

  • the Δ-boundary is the outer triangular enclosure, fixing the scope of the representation;
  • the internal triangulation consists of the quality-state vertices and the triangles their relations form.

The observer sits inside the boundary. The two levels must not be confused.

Principle 26.9 (The boundary is not the object). The Δ-boundary selects a scope. It does not declare what lies outside to be absent or irrelevant. A boundary may be widened, narrowed, subdivided or redrawn, and one subject may have several bounded representations: as an object, as an assembly, as a functioning system, as a component of a larger process, or as a system interacting with its environment. The identity reference stays fixed while the boundary changes.

27. Recursion, the Complex and the Manifold

Definition 27.1 (Recursive refinement). A triangle may be subdivided into smaller triangles carrying finer distinctions. Refining a single line of distinction is the one-dimensional case: halving its resolution step (k → 2k) doubles the positions it can distinguish. Refinement continues only while the residual deviation stays at ε or above (Definition 26.4). The power to subdivide does not imply that a subject has infinitely many intrinsic divisions.

Principle 27.2 (The Sierpiński reference). Sierpiński-style self-similar subdivision is a model for representing relational information at multiple scales. It is a representational technique, not a claim that reality is fractal.

Definition 27.3 (Complex and manifold). Triangles joined at shared vertices and edges form a triangulated complex. The complex qualifies as a surface manifold (a 2-manifold) only if two conditions hold:

  1. every edge belongs to exactly one or two triangles;
  2. the link of every vertex is a single circle (for an interior vertex) or a single arc (for a boundary vertex).

Where either condition fails, the structure keeps the name triangulated complex.

Principle 27.4 (Distinctions preserved). The following are distinct and must not be used interchangeably:

  • a triangular representation;
  • a triangulated complex;
  • a recursive triangular structure;
  • a fractal construction;
  • a manifold;
  • a manifold embedded in another space.

Hypothesis 27.5 (The folded manifold). Polarity is pendulation within a folded, layered or multidimensional relational structure, in which a change in one quality bends the relations among others. As a statement about the organization of state relations, this is a geometric hypothesis. Its link to physical spacetime is a further hypothesis that needs independent evidence.

Hypothesis 27.6 (Ripple). A pendulating primitive is the observable trace of higher-dimensional pattern: the peak or trough of a ripple. A subject is then a superposition of ripples, a wave packet of qualities. This is held apart from the representational system (§37).

28. The Observer

Definition 28.1 (Observer state). The observer is represented by an observer-state collection

O = [o₁, …, o_m],

which may include its position in the representation, available information, measurement capability, descriptive limitations, interpretive assumptions, temporal pointer τ, known uncertainties and relations to the subject. Including the observer does not equate the observer with the subject.

Principle 28.2 (Non-omniscience). An observer never holds the subject's full state, only its own reconstruction of it. Every record is stamped with τ, and four cases are kept apart:

  1. the subject changed;
  2. the reading changed;
  3. the subject changed without being noticed;
  4. the reading changed while the subject did not.

Definition 28.3 (Observational displacement). For observers a and b on quality q,

δ_q = p_q(a) − p_q(b).

A non-zero displacement is data. It must be attributed to one of the following sources: a change in the subject, a difference in access, resolution, reference, timing, framework or assumption, or incompatible definitions. It is never averaged away.

Principle 28.4 (Recursive observation). The observer can itself become a subject. An agent examining its own reasoning, a machine monitoring its own operations, or an organism within its environmental model each enters the representation as a component distinct from what it observes.

Principle 28.5 (Limits of observation).

  • Unknown qualities are not absent qualities.
  • Unmeasured magnitudes are not zero.
  • Unobserved relations are not non-existent.

Principle 28.6. Observation establishes distinguishability. It does not create physical reality.

29. Comparison and Synesthetic Abstraction

Definition 29.1 (Relations between descriptions). These seven relations are distinct and are never substituted for one another:

  • Identity: two descriptions refer to the same subject or property.
  • Equivalence: two structures satisfy a defined equivalence relation.
  • Similarity: two structures share selected characteristics.
  • Analogy: two structures correspond in a limited respect.
  • Association: two qualities are related within a model.
  • Correlation: two measured variables show a statistical relation.
  • Causation: a mechanism or causal relation has been established.

Definition 29.2 (Synesthetic abstraction). Synesthetic abstraction is the structured translation of qualities from different descriptive domains into a common relational representation, preserving each source definition. It is a representational operation and does not require neurological synesthesia. Examples:

  • thermal intensity ↔ informational intensity;
  • structural rigidity ↔ conceptual rigidity;
  • physical expansion ↔ expansion of a representational space;
  • signal frequency ↔ recurrence in a temporal sequence;
  • material permeability ↔ informational accessibility;
  • structural stability ↔ persistence of a relational configuration.

Such pairings assert shared structure, not physical identity.

Definition 29.3 (Alignment). Two primitives are aligned when their lines of distinction have identical endpoint definitions. Only aligned positions are compared directly.

Definition 29.4 (Cross-domain map). A proposed map F from the lines of domain A to those of domain B is structure-preserving when it does three things:

  1. sends limits to limits (0 to 0 and 1 to 1, or consistently reversed);
  2. carries relations to relations;
  3. carries pendulation to pendulation.

Every map records what is translated, what is unchanged, what is lost, what is added, what is uncertain, and which correspondences have been independently established.

Definition 29.5 (The comparison operator). A comparison is written

C(A, B | ref, F),

naming its reference and its mapping. It returns a triple rather than a single number:

  • match: structure that survives the mapping;
  • mismatch: structure that breaks;
  • uncomparable: what has no counterpart.

It also states which characteristics were compared and which were excluded.

Standard 29.6 (Null baseline). A correspondence is asserted only if it outperforms a random mapping on the same data. Otherwise the analogy is decorative.

Principle 29.7 (Translation without identity collapse). Two qualities can share relational structure without being the same phenomenon. Physical and conceptual rigidity share resistance to change, but their mechanisms and measurements differ. Synesthetic abstraction exposes the shared structure and erases neither source.

30. Worked Examples

30.1 A descriptor

Subject: a dog. Reference: one observer, daylight, visual inspection. Resolution k = 100.

  • Fur — 0 := hairless; 1 := maximally dense coat. p = 0.90.
  • Brown — 0 := no brown hue; 1 := fully brown. p = 0.67.
  • Red — 0 := no red hue; 1 := fully red. p = 0.54.

Signed: s = (0.80, 0.34, 0.08). Phases: φ ≈ (36.9°, 70.1°, 85.4°). Deviations: sin φ ≈ (0.60, 0.94, 1.00).

Fur is nearly decided. Red sits almost at the midpoint and is maximally open, which is where further observation, such as lighting or breed, would most change the description. Brown and red are independent lines, not complements, and both can be high together.

30.2 A relational quality: wetness

Wetness is fixed by three interfacial tensions, related by Young's equation:

γ_SV = γ_SL + γ_LV cos θ,

where SV is solid–vapour, SL solid–liquid, LV liquid–vapour, and θ is the contact angle. Wetness is a triangle by construction. Define L_wet with 0 := complete non-wetting (θ = 180°) and 1 := complete spreading (θ = 0°). Then p = (1 + cos θ)/2. This is exactly the phase form of §24, with the contact angle as the phase.

  • Mercury on glass, θ ≈ 140°: p ≈ 0.12.
  • Mercury on clean gold (it wets and amalgamates), θ → 0°: p → 1.
  • Water on clean glass, θ ≈ 0–20°: p ≈ 0.97–1.00.

The same liquid shows opposite wetness. The quality belongs to the configuration of liquid, surface and medium. On gold, mercury is wet in exactly the sense that water is wet on glass. This is a structure-preserving cross-domain map (Definition 29.4).

30.3 Deviation

Take the axis u = (1, 0) and a new quality v at θ = 60°.

  • Polarity component: cos 60° = 0.500.
  • Deviation component: sin 60° ≈ 0.866.

With ε = 0.1, v opens a new axis. At θ = 3°, the deviation is ≈ 0.052 < ε, so that quality is redundant.

30.4 The interior of a triangle

Take three qualities of a material: integrity, flexibility and density. A sample whose behaviour is judged 50% integrity-like, 30% flexibility-like and 20% density-like sits at barycentric coordinates (0.5, 0.3, 0.2) in the interior. The interior point is the weighted combined condition. Moving toward a vertex means that quality increasingly dominates the joint behaviour.

30.5 Compactification

Temperature excess above room temperature, with x₀ = 60 °C:

  • 0 °C excess → p = 0;
  • 60 °C → p = 0.5;
  • 80 °C → p ≈ 0.57;
  • 600 °C → p ≈ 0.91.

No finite temperature reaches p = 1. The is-pole remains the infinite limit.

30.6 Observational displacement

Two observers rate "red" on the same dog: observer a gives 0.54 in daylight, observer b gives 0.71 under tungsten light. Then δ_red = −0.17. This is attributed to a difference in reference (illumination), not to the dog, and both records are kept.

30.7 Orthogonal investigation

The claim "Mercury is wet" and the claim "Mercury is not wet" appear to contradict each other. Under the original framing, wetness is a property of a liquid, and the contradiction cannot be resolved. Changing the framing so that wetness is a relation of liquid, surface and medium (Example 30.2) dissolves it. Both statements are true of different configurations. The contradiction belonged to the framework, not to the subject.

31. The Quality Scripture

Definition 31.1 (Scripture). The Scripture Σ is the shared, persistent set of quality definitions. Each entry contains:

  • name and meaning;
  • measurement procedure;
  • Monomaxum endpoints, or a note that the quality has no polarity;
  • domain and provenance;
  • known relations to other entries.

Principle 31.2. New subjects reuse existing entries where they fit and add new entries only where the Scripture is silent. The Scripture expands without losing the consistency of what it already holds.

32. Orthogonal Abstraction

Principle 32.1 (Representation is not bound to its first language). An agent need not solve a problem in the language in which it was presented. It may do any of the following:

  • change the selected qualities;
  • introduce dimensions;
  • replace an unsuitable polarity axis;
  • construct alternative triangulations;
  • compare relational structures;
  • expand the Δ-boundary;
  • separate combined variables or combine separate ones;
  • translate between domains.

Definition 32.2 (Fork and compare).

  1. Copy the current representation.
  2. In each copy, change one dimension of the description.
  3. Test every copy against the evidence.
  4. Record the verdicts.

Rejected alternatives are retained, because knowing what failed is part of knowing the subject.

Principle 32.3 (Locating contradiction). The purpose of orthogonal investigation is not to evade contradiction. It is to determine whether the contradiction belongs to the subject, the model, the observer or the framework (Example 30.7).

Principle 32.4 (Orthogonal comparison). Systems unrelated in their native languages may share operational organization: inputs, internal state, transformations, dependencies, constraints, outputs and feedback. Such sharing does not make them identical, but it exposes relations hidden within each native framework.

Principle 32.5 (Undecidability within a model). Some problems, such as an observer determining the complete nature of an environment it is wholly inside, may be unsolvable under their assumptions. The instrument can represent the observer, the system, the available information, the boundaries of observation, possible external references and alternative explanations. It can then determine whether the problem is solvable, needs more information, or is undecidable within the model.

Principle 32.6 (Abstraction is generative). Abstraction is not just the removal of detail. It is construction. An object is abstracted into structural qualities, those qualities into relational patterns, and the patterns are compared with another subject, which may yield a new way to describe either one. Every abstraction keeps its link to the subject it came from.

33. The Unitary Instrument and the Indefinite Agent

Principle 33.1 (Unitary means operational unity). "Unitary" does not mean reducing subjects to one number, imposing one measurement procedure, or erasing distinctions. It means coordinating different representational functions within one instrument that keeps each kind of information's meaning while giving all of them a shared structure.

Principle 33.2 (Extensibility). An agent whose domain is not known in advance needs extensibility at every level:

  • subjects;
  • qualities;
  • domains;
  • measurement procedures;
  • polarity axes;
  • relational operators;
  • triangulations;
  • abstraction mappings;
  • reconstruction procedures.

It must not depend on a fixed catalogue of objects.

Principle 33.3 (Beginning without completeness). The instrument works from partial and uncertain information. It distinguishes known, suspected, inferred, hypothetical, unmeasured and unknown qualities, and it lets incomplete structures stay incomplete until information arrives. A subject may begin as an image, a sound, an observation, a description, a symbol or an impression, and the instrument turns it into structure without requiring the final mathematical language in advance.

Definition 33.4 (Operational procedure).

  1. Introduce the subject, in whatever form, however incomplete.
  2. Establish the identity reference and the Δ-boundary: what is inside the representation and what is outside it.
  3. Situate the observer: position, information and limits.
  4. Differentiate qualities from observation, measurement, definition, comparison or hypothesis.
  5. Classify each quality: quantitative, qualitative, categorical, relational, temporal, structural, functional, perceptual, informational, inferred or unresolved.
  6. Assign magnitude where meaningful, with reference conditions. Normalize only where normalization preserves meaning.
  7. Assign polarity where a meaningful axis exists, defining both Monomaxum points. Otherwise use an alternative structure.
  8. Record each primitive in the quality record and the Scripture.
  9. Relate qualities, distinguishing association, dependency, opposition, correlation, influence and causation.
  10. Compare synesthetically across domains, preserving source meanings.
  11. Triangulate, justifying each triangle and defining its vertices, edges and interior.
  12. Check manifold conditions, or keep the name complex.
  13. Refine recursively where residual deviation remains.
  14. Observe pendulation: the change of positions over time.
  15. Investigate orthogonally where contradictions arise.
  16. Verify and reconstruct: record assumptions, uncertainties, counterexamples and revisions, so that the representation can be rebuilt from its definitions and operations.

Purpose 33.5 (Self-establishing intelligence). The primitive is designed so that an intelligence can establish itself from quality descriptors of its own self-reference. It begins with its own Δ = Δ, describes itself in primitives, and extends its description by deviation. The compound structure of such an intelligence is developed in Part Two.

34. Correspondence with χ-RCC

The χ-Reentrant Closure Calculus is one formal lens on the primitive. Its proven results support the primitive's structure. Its conjectures remain conjectures, and none of its results establishes the philosophical claims.

The proto-atomos is J. The proposed operator J (Interject) is a differentiation whose daughter returns to its source:

s → s′ → s.

This is Δ = Δ in operational form: a distinction made and returned from in a single act. J is the constructive form of Axiom A2 (recurrence), because it creates a recurrent pair in one step. In the reference implementation, every change the kernel admits is a paired transition of exactly this kind.

Correspondences:

  • PA1, Δ = Δ: A2, made constructive by J.
  • A stable primitive: a closed recurrent class C, which by Theorem 3 satisfies D(C) = C.
  • The two Monomaxum points: two fibers over one base in a discrete fibered graph. This is discrete, not a differential-geometric bundle.
  • Establishing the two points: D (Differentiate).
  • The relation that closes a triangle: C (Couple), which induces mutual reachability.
  • A retained but inactive primitive (⊥): S (Suspend).
  • Admissibility of operations on a primitive: the χ-field, its differentiation quota, and the forbidden compositions (S, A) and (S, C).
  • Pendulation: directional flux. Coherence is κ = Σ|N_f − N_r| ⁄ Σ(N_f + N_r), and balance is β = 1 − κ.
  • Polarity between two trajectories: (same − opposite) ⁄ (same + opposite), which lies in [−1, +1].
  • The interior observer (PA6): A1 (closure) together with Theorem 3 (observation as fixed point).
  • The record of construction (§36): the hash-chained history of kernel decisions.

Witness. The implementation replays 300 legacy scenarios identically, blocks unauthorized changes, detects tampering, and verifies Theorems 1, 1b and 3 on 2,459 recurrent random digraphs with no counterexamples. This establishes that the operations are consistent and executable. It does not test the philosophical claims.

35. Reliability and Validity

Standard 35.1 (Definitions precede comparison).

  • Every quality keeps its definition.
  • Every magnitude keeps its measurement conditions.
  • Every polarity keeps its endpoints.
  • Every relation keeps its meaning.
  • Every triangle keeps the definitions of its vertices and edges.

Without these, the instrument produces representations that are geometrically organized but semantically unreliable.

Standard 35.2 (Forms of evaluation). Five forms are kept distinct:

  1. Formal validity: the construction satisfies its definitions and axioms.
  2. Representational validity: it describes its subject faithfully.
  3. Observational support: positions agree with observation within resolution.
  4. Inferential support: conclusions follow from the definitions and premises.
  5. Empirical confirmation: independent evidence supports a physical interpretation.

Standard 35.3 (Context in comparison). Equal magnitudes are not equivalent qualities. Equal polarity values may sit on different axes. Congruent triangles may represent different relations. Structurally similar manifolds may represent different subjects.

Standard 35.4 (Unknowns remain unknown). No missing value is invented to complete a pleasing structure. An unmeasurable magnitude stays unspecified. An unjustified polarity is not forced. An uncertain relation is marked uncertain. An incomplete geometry stays incomplete.

Standard 35.5 (Explicit relations). Geometric proximity does not establish a relation. Every edge corresponds to a defined, typed relation, and visual adjacency is never silently read as causation.

36. Reproducibility

Every representation preserves its construction history:

  • the initial subject description;
  • original observations;
  • qualities identified;
  • definitions;
  • measurement conditions;
  • polarity endpoints;
  • relational assignments;
  • triangulation operations;
  • subdivisions;
  • cross-domain mappings;
  • transformations;
  • assumptions;
  • uncertainties;
  • revisions;
  • verification results.

A representation that cannot be traced to its definitions and operations is not reliable enough for the instrument's purpose. In the χ-RCC implementation, the history is append-only and hash-chained:

h₀ = GENESIS, hᵢ = SHA-256(hᵢ₋₁ ‖ recordᵢ).

37. Physical Interpretation

The framework is useful for describing abstract relations, information structures and state transformations, whether or not any physical interpretation is confirmed. On its own, it does not establish any of the following:

  • that physical spacetime is a Sierpiński structure;
  • that qualities literally oscillate between metaphysical endpoints;
  • that the folded manifold is physical;
  • that polarity is a ripple of higher-dimensional pattern;
  • that the primitive is a fundamental physical field.

These remain separate hypotheses (27.5, 27.6) and require independent evidence. The representational primitive does not depend on them.

38. Open Interfaces and Criteria for Refutation

This conjecture is meant to be completed by many contributions, both mathematical and philosophical, and not closed by any single one. Infinity enters it as a horizon, not a computation.

Refutation. The primitive would be weakened or refuted by any of the following:

  1. a distinguishable quality that cannot be represented on any bounded line of distinction, or by any finite set of such lines;
  2. structure-preserving correspondences whose preservation fails to predict which transferred inferences are correct;
  3. a structure requiring a primitive that cannot be decomposed into polarity and deviation;
  4. a demonstration that observer-displacement records carry no more information than noise.

Open interfaces:

  • canonical measurement procedures for each class of quality;
  • formal treatment of multi-pole and categorical qualities;
  • a metric of structure-preservation across domains;
  • the physical status of the ripple and of the folded manifold;
  • the bound relating resolution k to the observer's window (Principle 24.4).

40. Conclusion

Between Zero and Mono-Maximum lies the line of distinction, and on it the proto-atomos: one quality, two limits, one position, one reference, inside the system it describes. Its polarity places every quality between the infinitesimal and the infinite. Its phase joins polarity and deviation in a single angle. Its deviation closes the first triangle. Its pendulation traces the ripple through the fold. Its observer always stands within it.

On this primitive rest a scripture of qualities, a discipline of comparison that does not collapse identity, and a method of investigation that changes the framing when the problem demands it. Together they form a unitary instrument through which an agent can meet the unfamiliar and begin to understand it before it has the language to explain it.

The qualities of a thing become the components of its representation. Their magnitudes and polarities describe their states. Their relations establish their geometry, and their changes establish their history.

The conjecture is that this one primitive is the unit from which all describable structure can be assembled. Part Two develops that assembly, the lattice of many primitives.
