THE EMOTIONAL SURROGACY CONJECTURE

MAKURABA MACHINE PSYCHOLOGY NATURAL LANGUAGE PROCESSING

THE EMOTIONAL SURROGACY CONJECTURE

A Computational Hypothesis Concerning Affective Tokenization, Vectorial Semantic Reconstruction, and Contextual Emotional Processing

ABSTRACT

The Emotional Surrogacy Conjecture proposes that natural language processing systems require an adequate computational representation of affective information to interpret language whose semantic structures are influenced by emotional associations, psychological conditions, and contextual emotional implications.

The conjecture originates from the observation that natural language is not exclusively composed of lexically identifiable symbols. Its development, application, and interpretation are substantially influenced by the psychological and social conditions of the individuals and populations using it.

Consequently, linguistic tokens may carry associations that extend beyond their immediate lexical definitions. These associations can include emotional valence, intensity, psychological appraisal, social implications, experiential conditioning, and contextual expectations.

The conjecture proposes that an artificial language-processing architecture should therefore incorporate an emotional surrogate mechanism capable of representing, evaluating, and transforming affective information throughout the tokenization and contextual interpretation process.

This mechanism need not establish subjective emotional experience within the processing system. Rather, it would provide a computational approximation of the affective relationships that contribute to the construction and interpretation of linguistic meaning.

A central component of this proposal is the use of sequential affective vectors to model the transformation of emotional token associations into an integrated contextual semantic state, designated S.

The resulting architecture would allow emotional information to participate in semantic reconstruction rather than remain an isolated classification applied after linguistic interpretation.

I. FOUNDATIONAL PROPOSITION

Natural language is a psychologically conditioned symbolic system.

Although individual words can be assigned conventional lexical definitions, their interpretation is not necessarily independent of the psychological, social, and emotional circumstances surrounding their application.

A word may communicate an immediate identifiable concept while simultaneously activating secondary associations that influence its interpretation.

For example:

HOME

Potential lexical interpretation: A place of residence.

Potential affective associations: Security. Belonging. Familiarity. Nostalgia. Isolation. Separation. Grief. Comfort.

These associations are not necessarily inherent, universal, or simultaneously activated.

Their relevance depends upon the contextual conditions under which the token is processed.

Therefore, the conjecture proposes that linguistic tokens should be considered as potentially multidimensional semantic objects whose interpretive properties extend beyond their lexical definitions.

A language-processing system that represents only lexical and syntactic relationships may consequently fail to reconstruct some of the contextual information necessary for accurately interpreting an utterance.

The proposed emotional surrogate mechanism addresses this deficiency by introducing an additional computational representation of affective information.

II. DEFINITION OF THE CONTEXTUAL SEMANTIC STATE (S)

For the purposes of this conjecture, S designates the contextual semantic state produced through the integration of lexical, syntactic, affective, pragmatic, and situational information.

S is not defined as an individual word, an isolated emotion, or a predetermined interpretation.

S represents the resulting contextual interpretation of an entire linguistic sequence at a particular processing stage.

We can define its conceptual composition as:

S = F(L, C, A, P, I)

Where:

S = Contextual semantic state.

L = Lexical information.

C = Linguistic and situational context.

A = Affective information.

P = Pragmatic information.

I = Inferred communicative intention.

F = The computational integration function responsible for combining these dimensions.

The contextual semantic state is dynamic rather than necessarily permanent.

As additional tokens, sentences, or contextual observations become available, S may be revised.

For example:

"That's wonderful."

The initial semantic state may favor a positive interpretation.

However, additional information could substantially alter that interpretation.

"That's wonderful. Another three hours of unpaid work."

The second sentence introduces contextual information that may shift the interpretation toward sarcasm, frustration, or dissatisfaction.

Therefore:

S(initial) ≠ S(updated)

The semantic state is dependent upon the accumulated information available to the processing system.

III. THE EMOTIONAL TOKENIZATION HYPOTHESIS

The conjecture proposes that each token may be represented through multiple associated informational dimensions.

Let T represent an individual linguistic token.

Its computational representation may be described as:

T = [L, V, A, D, R, K]

Where:

L = Lexical identity and conventional semantic content.

V = Emotional valence.

A = Emotional arousal or activation.

D = Dominance or perceived agency within the represented emotional condition.

R = Relational affective associations.

K = Contextual dependency and interpretive uncertainty.

These dimensions constitute a proposed representational framework rather than a claim that every token possesses fixed emotional values.

For example, the token:

"Wonderful"

May initially receive a positive valence association.

However, its contextual affective representation remains subject to modification.

The token may communicate appreciation, sarcasm, disbelief, frustration, or even hostility.

Its lexical identity remains recognizable while its affective interpretation changes.

This establishes an important distinction:

LEXICAL STABILITY DOES NOT NECESSARILY PRODUCE AFFECTIVE STABILITY.

IV. THE AFFECTIVE VECTOR CHAIN

The central computational proposal involves representing the sequential contribution of individual tokens to the construction of S.

Let an utterance consist of an ordered sequence of tokens:

T1 → T2 → T3 → T4 → Tn

Each token contributes information to the evolving contextual interpretation.

The affective representation of each token is designated:

E(Ti)

Where E represents the affective feature vector associated with token Ti.

The processing architecture evaluates the individual vectors in conjunction with previously accumulated information.

The sequential relationship can be represented as:

S0 → S1 → S2 → S3 → Sn

Where:

S0 = Initial contextual state.

S1 = Contextual state following the first token.

S2 = Contextual state following the second token.

S3 = Contextual state following the third token.

Sn = Resulting contextual state following the complete sequence.

Each transition is represented as:

Si = F(Si-1, Ti, E(Ti), Ci)

Where:

Si = Updated contextual semantic state.

Si-1 = Previously established contextual semantic state.

Ti = Current token.

E(Ti) = Affective vector associated with the current token.

Ci = Available contextual information at the current processing stage.

F = Contextual integration and transformation function.

The importance of this structure is that the emotional interpretation of an individual token is not necessarily preserved unchanged throughout the sequence.

Its contribution can be amplified, suppressed, contradicted, or reinterpreted through subsequent contextual information.

This is the proposed VECTOR CHAIN OF AFFECTIVE SEMANTIC RECONSTRUCTION.

V. EMOTIONAL VECTOR HEAT MAPPING

To establish a more explicit computational representation, the conjecture proposes an affective heat map.

The heat map represents the relative activation of affective dimensions throughout a linguistic sequence.

For this example, consider the following simplified dimensions:

V = Valence. A = Arousal. D = Dominance. U = Uncertainty.

Each token receives an illustrative vector:

E(Ti) = [V, A, D, U]

For demonstration, values are normalized between -1 and +1 for V and between 0 and 1 for the remaining dimensions.

These values are hypothetical model outputs, not universal measurements of human emotional experience.

Consider the sentence:

"That's just wonderful."

TOKEN 1: THAT'S

Affective vector: [0.00, 0.05, 0.00, 0.10]

Interpretation: Minimal independent affective contribution. Primarily establishes reference to an existing subject or circumstance.

TOKEN 2: JUST

Affective vector: [0.00, 0.10, 0.05, 0.15]

Interpretation: Introduces qualification and potentially modifies the intensity or scope of the subsequent expression.

TOKEN 3: WONDERFUL

Affective vector: [+0.85, 0.55, 0.15, 0.10]

Interpretation: Conventional positive affective association. Potentially communicates appreciation or approval.

At this stage, a system relying heavily on lexical associations may produce a positive interpretation.

However, this is not necessarily the final contextual interpretation.

The affective vector chain must remain responsive to additional contextual information.

VI. CONTEXTUAL VECTOR TRANSFORMATION

Now introduce the surrounding circumstances:

The speaker has just discovered that another three hours of unpaid work are required.

The complete utterance becomes:

"That's just wonderful. Another three hours of unpaid work."

The second sentence introduces new information.

The expression "wonderful" may now be interpreted ironically.

The original positive lexical association is not erased.

Instead, its contribution is reconsidered in relation to the newly available contextual information.

The resulting affective transformation might resemble:

INITIAL LEXICAL ASSOCIATION:

V = +0.85

CONTEXTUAL CONTRADICTION:

V = -0.65

UPDATED AFFECTIVE INTERPRETATION:

V = -0.55

The resulting interpretation could involve frustration, sarcasm, or dissatisfaction.

The numerical values above are illustrative.

The important computational proposition is that the affective representation associated with a token may be transformed through contextual interaction.

We can express this as:

E(Ti | C1) ≠ E(Ti | C2)

Where:

E(Ti | C1) = Affective interpretation of token Ti under context C1.

E(Ti | C2) = Affective interpretation of the same token under context C2.

The same token can therefore produce different affective contributions depending on the contextual state in which it appears.

This is the proposed CONTEXTUAL AFFECTIVE TRANSFORMATION PRINCIPLE.

VII. THE SURROGACY FACTOR

The conjecture introduces a proposed computational quantity designated Ψ (Psi).

Ψ represents the degree to which an artificial processing architecture incorporates affective information into its contextual semantic reconstruction.

It is not a measurement of machine consciousness, genuine emotional experience, or subjective feeling.

Rather, it describes the functional contribution of affective representations to the interpretation process.

Conceptually:

Ψ = G(E, C, S, W)

Where:

E = Affective token representations.

C = Contextual information.

S = Evolving contextual semantic state.

W = The relative weighting and integration of affective information.

A system with an absent or insufficient affective representation may assign excessive interpretive importance to conventional lexical associations.

A system with a functioning emotional surrogate may instead evaluate the relationship between lexical meaning and contextual affective information.

The surrogacy factor therefore concerns the computational adequacy of affective representation.

It does not imply that the machine experiences the represented emotional condition.

An important qualification is that increasing Ψ indiscriminately would not necessarily improve interpretation.

Incorrect affective assumptions could introduce additional errors.

The objective is accurate affective contextualization, not maximal emotional activation.

VIII. THE COMPOSITION OF AFFECTIVE SEMANTIC MEANING

The conjecture proposes that contextual meaning may emerge through the interaction of several contributing informational dimensions.

These include:

  1. Lexical meaning.
  2. Syntactic relationships.
  3. Affective associations.
  4. Emotional intensity.
  5. Pragmatic implication.
  6. Social conditioning.
  7. Experiential associations.
  8. Situational context.
  9. Communicative intention.
  10. Interpretive uncertainty.

These components should not necessarily be treated as independent variables.

Their relationships may be nonlinear, reciprocal, and context-dependent.

For example, an apparently positive expression may acquire a negative interpretation when accompanied by contextual evidence of disappointment.

Similarly, an apparently hostile expression may function as affectionate teasing between individuals who share an established social relationship.

Therefore, emotional meaning cannot reliably be reconstructed through a universal dictionary of positive and negative words.

It requires relational interpretation.

IX. THE EMOTIONAL SURROGACY REQUIREMENT

The principal conjecture can now be stated more precisely:

A natural language processing architecture intended to achieve sufficiently comprehensive contextual interpretation of affectively conditioned human language should incorporate an affective representation and processing mechanism capable of modeling the emotional associations, contextual transformations, and pragmatic implications that contribute to linguistic meaning.

The proposed mechanism should:

A. Recognize potentially affective token associations.

B. Distinguish conventional lexical meaning from contextual affective meaning.

C. Represent emotional information through multidimensional feature vectors.

D. Maintain sequential relationships between affective token representations.

E. Update affective interpretations as additional contextual information becomes available.

F. Recognize contradictions between lexical expression and contextual implication.

G. Account for uncertainty when emotional interpretation cannot be established reliably.

H. Integrate affective information into the evolving contextual semantic state.

I. Avoid treating inferred emotional conditions as established facts about the speaker.

J. Preserve distinctions between computational affective modeling and subjective emotional experience.

X. FALSIFIABILITY AND EXPERIMENTAL VALIDATION

The conjecture must remain open to empirical examination.

It proposes the following testable hypothesis:

An NLP architecture incorporating an integrated affective-context processing mechanism will demonstrate measurable improvements in selected contextual interpretation tasks compared with an otherwise comparable architecture lacking that mechanism.

Experimental comparisons could include:

  1. Sarcasm recognition.
  2. Irony interpretation.
  3. Emotional ambiguity.
  4. Metaphorical expressions.
  5. Contextual polarity reversal.
  6. Concealed emotional meaning.
  7. Affectionate versus hostile language.
  8. Contradictory affective signals.
  9. Pragmatic interpretation.
  10. Context-dependent communicative intention.

The experiment should compare systems with equivalent underlying language capabilities, training conditions, and computational resources as far as practicable.

Evaluation should include both interpretation accuracy and calibration of uncertainty.

An affective mechanism that confidently invents emotions would not constitute a successful implementation.

Furthermore, if an architecture without a separately identifiable emotional surrogate achieves equivalent performance through distributed representations, this would challenge the necessity of a distinct surrogate subsystem.

It would not necessarily disprove the broader proposition that affective information contributes to contextual linguistic interpretation.

This distinction is important for maintaining scientific precision.

XI. LIMITATIONS AND BOUNDARY CONDITIONS

The conjecture does not establish that:

  • Every word possesses an emotional component.
  • Every linguistic expression communicates an emotional state.
  • Emotional interpretations are universal across individuals or cultures.
  • Computational affective representations constitute subjective feelings.
  • An artificial system must experience human emotions to process human language.
  • A dedicated emotional subsystem is the only possible implementation.
  • Affective information alone is sufficient to reconstruct communicative intention.

The proposal instead concerns the computational representation and integration of affective information as one contributing dimension of semantic interpretation.

The distinction between recognizing emotional patterns and possessing emotional experiences must remain explicit.

XII. FINAL CONJECTURAL STATEMENT

Natural language develops and operates within psychological, social, and affectively conditioned environments.

Its tokens may consequently inherit associations that influence their interpretation beyond their conventional lexical definitions.

When these tokens are assembled into larger linguistic structures, their affective contributions interact with surrounding semantic, pragmatic, and situational information.

The resulting interpretation is therefore not necessarily recoverable through lexical identification and syntactic reconstruction alone.

The Emotional Surrogacy Conjecture proposes that sufficiently comprehensive natural language processing requires the computational modeling of these affective relationships, whether through a dedicated subsystem or through an equivalent integrated representational architecture.

Through sequential affective vector processing, a system may model the transformation of token-level emotional associations into an evolving contextual semantic state, designated S.

The proposed surrogacy mechanism supplies an informational bridge between linguistic symbols and the affective conditions that influence their interpretation.

It does not require the artificial system to experience the emotional conditions it represents.

It requires that the system adequately model their informational consequences.

FINAL PRINCIPLE:

A system cannot reliably reconstruct every dimension of a language's meaning while systematically disregarding dimensions of information that contribute to the formation and interpretation of that language.

The question is not necessarily whether a machine must feel what a human feels.

The question is whether it must computationally represent what feeling contributes to meaning.

END OF CONJECTURE
