01The idea in one page

An object is not only what it is. It is also a source of what it could become. Take a small green crab apple, Apple I. Measure its qualities: size, colour, shape, sweetness, markings. Each quality becomes a number, and the list of numbers becomes a point in a many-dimensional space. Change the numbers and you describe a different apple. Do it again from the new apple and you get a branching family of possibilities.

Three findings give the idea its scientific shape:

  1. Describable is far larger than realizable. Almost any list of numbers can be written down, but only a thin region of the space holds apples that could actually grow. In the app, only about 0.4 % of random lists land near real apples.
  2. Qualities are coupled. Changing one drags others with it, so the realizable region has a shape, and pushes can ripple or run away.
  3. Small steps limit what is reachable. Starting from one seed and taking bounded steps, you can only walk to the connected region around it.

The project is two things: a corrected statement of the conjecture, and Apple of Apples, an interactive HTML visualization that lets anyone, from a child to a researcher, see and play with each idea.

02Where it started

The project began with an illustrated manifesto, Mono-Maximum: "From One, Many · From Many, One." It showed Apple I as a seed, a branching tree of reconfigured apples (melon-striped, blue, gemstone, yarn, stone, plasma, tentacled), a Hilbert sphere of extractable qualities (the infographic's "Gilbert" is a transcription typo), and a process: observe, extract, configure, reconstruct, iterate. Its sharpest line was "Combinatorial (what we can describe) ≠ Realizable (what can exist)".

The work since then kept that spirit and fixed what wouldn't survive scrutiny:

Original ideaCorrection
"Bijection of nature"Describing an object loses information: many apples share one description. The model uses an encoder and decoder, and measures the gap.
"Hilbert sphere of apples"Made precise. A Hilbert space applies rigorously in two places: the apple's outline as a function (L²), and shape with size removed (a hypersphere).
Hand-picked "can't grow" rulesThe boundary is derived from data with a standard statistical distance.
"Triadic systems are inevitable", "Make correctness inevitable"Kept as framing, not claims. The second became a method: always separate what the model represents, demonstrates, and leaves unknown.

03The formal model

As k grows, Reach fills the connected part of V around the seed. The seed matters only because steps are bounded, and regions cut off by gaps wider than ε can never be reached from it.

The conjectureFor a domain with descriptor space F and realizable set V: (i) V is low-dimensional inside F; (ii) its branching follows measurable couplings between qualities; (iii) bounded-step reconstruction from a seed reaches exactly the connected region of V around it.

04Four levels, kept distinct

A central discussion of the project was what the vector actually captures. The resolution was a directive framework that keeps four levels separate without deciding in advance how they relate:

LevelWhat it isIn the model
1 · Qualitative dimensionsThe distinguishable qualities: redness, roundness, sweetness, size, texture. Coordinates of a quality space (Gärdenfors's "conceptual spaces").represented
2 · Configurational stateOne object's particular combination of those qualities: a state vector.represented
3 · Phenomenal experienceQualia in the strict sense: what it is like for an observer to encounter that state.not represented · open
4 · Observer relationshipWhether two observers can meet structurally equivalent states yet experience them differently.test case · open
Central principleThe model represents qualitative structure and configurational state. It does not, by itself, establish phenomenal experience. Representing a quality is not the same as experiencing it, but neither should experience be assumed to be understandable apart from the structures through which qualities become distinguishable.

05What can exist: V from data

An early version of the app used made-up rules ("Boxy above 0.6 can't grow"). They were arbitrary, and they were replaced. Now the boundary of V comes from a table of 14 reference apples, wild and red crab apples plus cultivars from Granny Smith to Bramley, each described with the same qualities in real units (diameter in mm, sugar in °Brix, height-to-width ratio).

The app fits the average μ and covariance Σ of those apples, then measures how far any apple sits from the group in units of the group's own spread, the Mahalanobis distance:

d(q) = √( (q − μ)ᵀ Σ⁻¹ (q − μ) ) ≤ 6.35 = √χ²22(0.99)

The cutoff is the standard point containing 99 % of a bell-shaped cloud in 22 dimensions (9 qualities, 6 wave genes, 7 exotic genes). Because Σ records which traits move together, an unusual combination can be unrealistic even when every number alone is normal. Each verdict names the closest real apple and the quality that pushed the apple out: "✅ like Red Delicious · d 3.1" or "❌ Boxy too high · d 16.7".

06The hypersphere

Centre each quality so the "average apple" sits at the origin. Then every apple has a length (how extreme it is) and a direction (what kind of apple it is). Dividing by the length places every apple on the unit hypersphere: the "sphere of apples", made precise.

xi = 2(qi − ½) ,  û = x / ‖x‖ ,  θ(a, b) = arccos( ûa · ûb )

Two further results connect to the sphere. In shape theory, outlines with position, rotation and size removed live on a hypersphere (Kendall). And in modern language models, normalisation layers (RMSNorm) put every token vector on a sphere of radius √d, which the capstone demonstrates exactly.

07Every apple is a wave

Read the apple's outline as one curve: its radius f(v) at each angle v from the stem (0°) to the bottom (180°). That curve is a single function, a point in the Hilbert space L², which is a genuine Hilbert space, not an analogy. Functions can be measured against each other:

⟨f, g⟩ = ∫₀^π f(v) g(v) sin v dv ,  θ = arccos( ⟨f, g⟩ / ‖f‖‖g‖ )

The curve also splits into Legendre waves Pℓ(cos v), the axially symmetric spherical harmonics. Their amounts aℓ are the apple's coordinates in that space: ℓ = 0 is overall size, ℓ = 1 is top-to-bottom lopsidedness, ℓ = 2 is tall versus flat, and higher ℓ is finer ripple. The shape is built the same way: a base curve multiplied by a wave, 1 + Σ ck cos(k·v), with six wave genes.

08Growth that never stops

  • φ-mutation. Each gene changes with probability 1/φ ≈ 0.618, and wave gene k changes by an amount that shrinks by a factor of φ for each step up in k, so coarse shape drifts more than fine ripple.
  • Projection. If a child lands outside V, it slides back along the line toward the average real apple until it reaches V's edge.
  • Two modes. 🍎 Real: growth is projected and stays realizable. 🌌 Trans-dimensional: all 23 genes mutate (including hue, metallic, glow, fatness, facets, spikes and texture patterns from the original infographic), nothing is projected, and the apple roams the whole describable space while its realism distance is still measured.

09Identity: state, Super ID, instance

Every apple displays its full state as a vector chain, grouped as qualities, waves, exotic genes and pattern:

S[0d.8c.66.08.20.00.33.80.4d | 87.7a.83.80.80.80 | d9.ff.1a.00.00.00.00 | 0]

State chain S

Every quality of the apple, written out.

Super ID

A UUID computed from S. Identical states always share it: it marks one point in the state space.

Local ID

The Super ID plus place and time, @(x, y, z | t): one particular instance.

Six state slots freeze a state together with an exact image of the view. Load a slot and compare it with the live apple, and the result is the project's identity demonstration: same state chain, same Super ID, state distance 0.000, yet different Local IDs in space and time. Identical in kind, distinct as objects: two tokens of one type.

ScopeThis describes classical, everyday objects, where position tells copies apart. Quantum particles of one kind are treated by physics as truly indistinguishable and carry no individual labels.

10Exploration and reversibility

Explore starts from Apple I and applies 14 mutators in a fixed cycle (shape, size, colour, marks, taste, wave, hue, metal, glow, fatness, facets, spikes, pattern, and a full random combination), with random size and direction within each, until every mutator and all six patterns have been used.

Retrace runs back along the lineage's own curved path to its root, passing smoothly through every in-between state; Replay runs it forward; a scrubber does either by hand, and growing from any point starts a new branch.

Why it is reversibleThe random mutations themselves cannot be undone by any formula. The walk is reversible because every child stores its parent: the path is a recorded history.

11Cascades in a vector medium

Genes are coupled, so a push to one spreads to the genes linked to it, then to theirs. With the state as a vector x and the couplings as a matrix A:

xt+1 = A xt ,  A = ρ · C / ρ(C)

The gain ρ is the size of A's largest eigenvalue, and it alone decides the regime: below 1 a ripple dies out, at 1 it persists, above 1 it runs away until genes saturate at their limits. Whatever gene is kicked, a long cascade lines up with A's top eigenvector, the pattern the medium amplifies most. This is the general behaviour of cascades through any vector medium or vector matrix, and the main idea behind exploding and vanishing signals in deep networks.

12The quantum analogy

Using the 14 reference apples as basis states, a quantum apple is a blend of all of them at once, each with a complex amplitude:

|ψ⟩ = Σ ci |i⟩ ,  Σ |ci|² = 1 ,  P(i) = |ci|² ,  U(t) = e−iHt
  • Interference: a Hadamard on pairs of apples, applied twice, restores the original probabilities exactly.
  • Unitary evolution: probability flows between similar apples (a quantum walk) with the total fixed at 1, and running time backwards returns exactly to the start.
  • Measurement: one apple is chosen with probability |ci|² and the state collapses. That step cannot be undone, the quantum counterpart to Retrace.
Honest scopeThis is an analogy, not a claim that apples are quantum. Real apples lose quantum coherence almost instantly. What carries over exactly is the mathematics: amplitudes, the Born rule, interference, reversible evolution and irreversible measurement.

13Observers and qualia

The inverted-spectrum thought experiment asks: could two observers share an identical structure of colour similarities while red looks to one the way cyan looks to the other? The app tests the structural half of that question. Observer B's colour coordinates are A's after an isometry (red ↦ 1 − red, hue ↦ hue + ½). Isometries preserve every distance, so A and B rank all 14 reference apples in exactly the same order, and the largest distance difference is 0. Structure alone cannot tell the two apart.

This is the qualia counterpart of the identity demonstration. The Super ID shows that two things can match exactly at levels 1 and 2 and still be two objects. The inverted spectrum asks whether two experiences could match exactly at levels 1 and 2 and still differ at level 3. Whether that is coherent is left open, deliberately: it should not be settled by terminology.

The question connects to live positions in philosophy and science: quality-space theories (Clark, Rosenthal), representationalism (Tye, Dretske), Integrated Information Theory's "qualia space" (Balduzzi and Tononi), and Chalmers's "hard problem".

14Capstone: apples as tokens

A language model turns each token into a vector and updates it layer by layer. The apples run through the same operations: the reference apples are the vocabulary, the lineage is the context window, and each apple's genes are its embedding.

a = softmax(x·Xᵀ/√d) X ,  h = x + a ,  n = h / rms(h) ,  P(j) = softmax(n·ej / (√d·τ))
  1. Attention: the newest apple weighs every apple in its context.
  2. Residual add: it adds what it gathered to its own vector.
  3. Normalisation: it is rescaled onto a sphere of radius √22 (measured at exactly 4.690).
  4. Prediction: it is scored against the vocabulary, and the next apple is sampled and appended, the way a model writes one token and then reads its own output.

Positional encoding closes the circle with identity: identical apples in the context share a token ID (like the Super ID), and adding position gives each a distinct vector (like the Local ID). At low temperature, generation falls into a repetition loop, the same failure real models show when sampling too greedily.

In this light, any apple can stand for a concept, a word, or a step in a chain of operations: a pathway that moves a vector from "what this is" toward "what comes next".

15Represents, demonstrates, unknown

Represents

Qualitative dimensions, configurational states, realizability from data, function-space shape, identity versus instance, coupling and cascades, transformer operations.

Demonstrates

Describable ≫ realizable. Bounded steps limit reach. States can match exactly yet be two instances. Structurally equivalent codings are indistinguishable from relational data. Gain decides ripple versus runaway.

Remains unknown

Whether subjective experience arises from a system's configuration, from its relationship with an observer, or from something neither accounts for.

16Assumptions, stated openly

  • Reference apples: approximate typical values for 14 cultivars, assembled from general knowledge, not a measured dataset. Real measurements can replace them and the boundary updates automatically.
  • Estimation settings: shrinkage 0.3 on off-diagonal covariance and a noise floor of 0.05 per quality, because 14 apples are few for many dimensions.
  • Priors for wave and exotic genes: no real-apple data exists for them, so their spreads are stated assumptions (waves 0.08·φ−(k−1); exotic genes centred at zero).
  • The golden ratio: a design choice for mutation rates, not a claim that apple variation follows φ.
  • Cascade couplings: measured correlations among the 9 qualities; stated toy values elsewhere.
  • Closure budgets: the 256-bit capacity, memory budget and erasure budget are adjustable illustrative values; the Landauer figures are true lower bounds at 300 K, far below what real hardware spends.
  • Quantum and transformer layers: faithful operations on simplified, untrained systems. They show what the mathematics does, not what nature or a trained model has learned.

18 · Part IIConjunctive closure

Part I asks what an object can become. Part II asks a deeper question: can a system stay coherent while depending on something outside itself? The Conjunctive Closure Engine (CCE) answers with three conditions that must hold at the same time, and the apple system turns out to be a working instance of all three.

ClosureQuestionIn the apple system
ℐ · InformationalCan the system represent and process its states within finite resources?One state is 22 genes × 8 bits + log₂6 = 178.6 bits, exactly the S chain. History must fit a memory budget.
𝒯 · ThermodynamicCan it sustain its organisation through energy and entropy exchange?Every irreversible erasure (an evicted record, an overwritten slot, a quantum measurement) costs ≥ kBT ln 2 per bit. Reversible steps cost nothing.
𝒪 · OntologicalCan it keep its foundational rules without deriving them from its own emergent behaviour?V's parameters (μ, Σ, cutoff) are A₀, fitted from external reference apples. Growth must not rewrite them.

The system and its host are kept apart: 𝒮 = (X, T, R, E, A) is the apple system, and 𝒢 = (Ω, Φ, ℬ) is the Genesis matrix, the substrate it runs on (the browser and device, their power, and boundary conditions such as screen, memory and WebGL support). 𝒮 depends on 𝒢 but cannot control it. The app already has a defined degradation state for one boundary condition: without WebGL it falls back to flat 2D drawing.

19Validity of the closure framework

Each element of the CCE was checked against established science. Most of it stands; two statements needed sharper formulation.

ElementVerdictNote
Three closures as joint conditionsvalidA definition, so it cannot be wrong, only more or less useful. Pairs can hold while the third fails; the app shows each failing alone.
Imin = log₂ NvalidMinimum fixed-length code for N distinguishable states. The actual average can be lower (Shannon entropy ≤ log₂ N).
Landauer bound kBT ln 2validApplies to logically irreversible erasure only, as the document says; confirmed experimentally (Bérut et al., 2012). At 300 K it is about 2.87 × 10⁻²¹ J per bit. Real computers dissipate roughly a billion times more.
ΔStotal ≥ 0, local order by exporting entropyvalidThe second law; the basis of self-organising "dissipative structures" (Prigogine).
Gödelian firewall → self-reference boundary controllervalid correctionGödel's theorems limit what formal systems can prove; they are not a mechanism. Recursion limits and budgets are the right engineering tools.
Et ⇏ A₀reformulateRead literally, "⇏" means "does not logically imply", which is not the intended rule. The intended rule is that emergent dynamics have no write access to A₀: A₀ is invariant under every transition, written Tt(A₀) = A₀ for all t.
𝒞(t) as a yes/no conjunctionextendValid, but a margin per condition (how far from its limit) lets the observer see failure coming. The app shows margins alongside the verdict.
Boundary observervalidA standard supervisory monitor in control theory. To avoid the regress of "who watches the watcher", it is kept read-only unless explicitly permitted to act, and every action is logged.
Genesis matrix as a hosting substratescopedAs the document itself notes, this is not a claim that physical reality is a simulation. Here 𝒢 is concrete: the device and browser.

20The Conjunctive Closure Conjecture

Conjecture (Conjunctive Closure for reconstructive systems)A recursive reconstruction process (Part I) can sustain indefinite, coherent exploration over an interval [0, τ] only if 𝒞(t) = ℐ(t) ∧ 𝒯(t) ∧ 𝒪(t) = 1 for all t in that interval. Moreover, the three conditions are coupled: (i) reversibility consumes information capacity, because retracing requires stored history; (ii) bounded capacity forces erasure, which consumes thermodynamic budget; and (iii) anchoring the realizable set to the system's own output contracts it, so ontological closure cannot be supplied from inside.
ℐ(t): I(𝒮) ≤ C𝒮  ·  𝒯(t): Σ bits erased × kBT ln 2 ≤ E𝒢  ·  𝒪(t): Tt(A₀) = A₀

Predictions, and what the app shows

#PredictionResult in the app
P9Reversibility costs memory: Retrace reaches back only to the oldest retained record.With a 20-record budget, older records were evicted and the retrace path shortened to the retained steps.
P10Bounded memory forces erasure, and erasure has a thermodynamic floor.Each eviction logged 178.6 bits and ≥ 5.1 × 10⁻¹⁹ J; the erasure budget was exceeded, 𝒯 failed and the observer suspended growth.
P11Self-anchoring contracts V (model collapse).After 40 self-anchored generations, total variance trace Σ fell from 0.270 to 0.057, about 79 % narrower.
P12The conditions are conjunctive: any one can fail while the others hold.𝒪 alone failed under self-anchoring; 𝒯 alone failed under erasure pressure; ℐ alone failed with the observer not permitted to compress.

P11 has a real-world counterpart: generative AI models trained repeatedly on their own outputs lose diversity and degrade, a documented effect called model collapse (Shumailov et al., 2024). The apple system reproduces it in miniature, which is the strongest evidence that ontological anchoring is a real constraint and not only a philosophical one.

The philosophical heart

Can a system establish the conditions of its own existence, or must those conditions remain external to it? Two partial answers come from established results, each within its own domain. In formal systems, Gödel's second incompleteness theorem shows that a consistent, sufficiently expressive system cannot prove its own consistency. And in the app, a realizable set fitted to the system's own output contracts. Both point toward external grounding. Neither settles the general question, which stays open.

21Method of integration

  1. Identify 𝒮 and 𝒢. Name the system's states, transitions, resources, exchanges and axioms, and separately the substrate it depends on.
  2. Quantify ℐ. Count the bits per state (log₂ of the number of distinguishable states) and the bits of history retained.
  3. Account for 𝒯. Classify every operation as reversible or irreversible; charge irreversible ones at ≥ kBT ln 2 per erased bit against an energy budget drawn from 𝒢.
  4. Fix A₀ and test 𝒪. List the foundational parameters, keep a fingerprint of them, and verify that no transition rewrites them. Distinguish a real change to an axiom from a change in how it is interpreted.
  5. Evaluate 𝒞(t) with margins. Report the joint verdict and each condition's distance from its limit.
  6. Add the observer. Edge-triggered detection, a named violated condition, an evidence record, and a defined response (compression, recovery, suspension or termination) taken only when permitted.
  7. Stress each condition alone to confirm they are conjunctive, then test the couplings (P9–P11).
