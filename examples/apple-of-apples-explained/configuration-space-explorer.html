One apple, many possible apples

Start with one small green apple. That is the red dot.

Every spot on the map is a different apple you could describe: bigger, smaller, sweeter, more sour.

Only the green part holds apples that can actually grow. Most of what you can imagine cannot exist.

Nature changes an apple only a little at a time. The blue part is everywhere you can get to in small steps.

The small green island on the right is real, but small steps cannot get there. Some possible things cannot be reached from where you start.

Try it: slide Step size up slowly. Watch for the moment the blue jumps across to the island.

Three ideas: describe, realize, reach

  1. Describe. Measure an apple's qualities and each one becomes a number. Here there are two: diameter and acidity. Two numbers make a point on a map, and the whole map is F, the space of descriptions.
  2. Realize. Biology, chemistry and physics rule most points out. The points that some real apple could match form the region V. V is much smaller than F.
  3. Reach. From a starting apple (the seed), take steps no longer than ε. Each new apple becomes the next starting point. After k steps you have reached the set shown in blue.

Two facts follow. First, the seed matters only when steps are limited: tick Unbounded jumps and everything in V lights up. Second, parts of V separated by a gap wider than ε stay unreached however many steps you take.

Describing an apple also loses information. Many different apples share the same two numbers, so you can't rebuild the exact apple from its description. That is why "pulling an apple out of an apple" is an estimate, not a perfect copy.

Try it: set k to 5, then 10, then ∞. The blue spreads like a ripple and stops at V's edges.

Morphospace, constraints and models

Precedent. In 1966 David Raup described coiled shells with three parameters and found that real species fill only a small part of the parameter space. This field is called theoretical morphospace, and this map is a toy version of it.

Why V has a shape. Traits are coupled. Mass grows roughly with the cube of diameter. One gene (MdMYB10) moves red colour in both skin and flesh, and acidity is largely controlled by one locus (Ma). Couplings make V a thin, curved region rather than a filled box. That is why the green band runs diagonally.

Why steps are bounded. Mutation and breeding change a lineage a little per generation, so evolution is a step-bounded walk through V. Disconnected regions of V need a large jump to reach: hybridization, a major mutation, or engineering.

Neural networks. A trained model is a point θ in a space of N weights. A pretrained model is the seed and fine-tuning is a bounded step. Research on intrinsic dimension, LoRA, mode connectivity and task vectors finds that useful models occupy low-dimensional, largely connected regions. That is the same picture in millions of dimensions.

Caveat. Weight space has symmetries: reorder a layer's neurons and the weights change while behavior stays identical. Distances that mean something are measured in behavior, or after aligning neurons.

Definitions

Object space O; descriptor space F = ℝn; encoder E: O → F (many-to-one); decoder R: F → O ∪ {∅}.

V = { x ∈ F : R(x) ≠ ∅ }
Δ(A) = dO( A, R(E(A)) ) ≥ 0
Reachk(x0, ε) = { xk : xi+1 ∈ V, ‖xi+1 − xi‖ ≤ ε }

Conjecture. (i) dim V ≪ dim F. (ii) The branching of V is set by measurable couplings between descriptors. (iii) Reach∞(x0, ε) equals the ε-connected component of V containing x0.

Implementation here. F = [0, 1]² is sampled on a 110 × 110 grid, with axes rescaled to diameter 20–100 mm and acidity 0–2.5 % malic acid. Reachk is computed by breadth-first search over grid cells in V, where neighbours are all cells within Euclidean radius ε. "Darker" means smaller BFS depth. Unbounded jumps set Reach = V.

Falsifiable for real systems: estimate intrinsic dimension of measured trait data (P1); measure trait co-movement under intervention (P2); search for targets unreachable under a fixed step budget (P3).

What this map is, and what it is not

The green region is an illustrative toy, shaped to show the principles. It is not measured apple data. The island in particular is a teaching device, not a claim about any real cultivar. A real V needs real measurements, many more than two axes, and an estimate of the measurement noise.

What does carry over to real systems is the structure: describable is bigger than realizable, couplings shape the realizable region, and bounded steps restrict what can be reached from a starting point. Each of these can be tested.
